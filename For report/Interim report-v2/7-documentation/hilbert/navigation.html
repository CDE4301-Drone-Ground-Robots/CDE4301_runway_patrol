1.0 Subsystem Scope & Operational Constraints

Dry Tarmac ($\mu = 0.80$) • 4WD Skid-Steer Envelope

1.1 Operational Environment: Airfield Runway Sweep on Dry Pavement ($\mu = 0.80$)

The autonomous Foreign Object Debris (FOD) remediation rover is engineered specifically for active airfield runway, taxiway, and apron infrastructure under statutory civil aviation regulations (Federal Aviation Administration, 2014 [2]; ASTM International, 2020 [1]; Shan et al., 2025 [12]). Runways feature standard geometric profiles of $45\text{ to }60\text{ m}$ in width and $3000\text{ to }4000\text{ m}$ in length, paved in grooved dense-graded asphalt or brushed Portland cement concrete exhibiting high surface friction ($\mu \approx 0.80$ under dry ambient conditions).

Operating on active runways imposes strict engineering boundaries: sweeps must execute rapidly within tight air traffic inter-arrival windows (requiring transit sprints $\ge 10.0\text{ km/h}$ or $2.78\text{ m/s}$), while retaining absolute millimeter-to-centimeter precision to reliably capture metallic fasteners, wire fragments, and tire chunks (Munyer et al., 2022 [10]).

●The Open Runway Scan-Matching Degeneracy Problem

Standard 2D LiDAR scan matching minimizes the point-to-plane residual error between successive laser scans:

$$E(\Delta \mathbf{x}) = \sum_{k=1}^K \left\| \mathbf{n}_k^T \Big( \mathbf{R}(\Delta \theta) \mathbf{p}_k + \Delta \mathbf{t} - \mathbf{q}_k \Big) \right\|^2$$

Along a $4000\text{ m}$ runway strip, vertical landmarks (buildings, walls) are absent beyond the runway edge markers. Consequently, the longitudinal spatial intensity gradient $\nabla I_x \approx 0$, causing the LiDAR scan-matching Hessian matrix $\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J}^T \mathbf{J}$ to suffer singular value collapse:

$$\text{det}(\mathbf{H}) = \lambda_x \lambda_y \lambda_\theta \approx 0 \implies \mathbf{\Sigma}_x = (\mathbf{H}^{-1})_{xx} \to \infty$$

Under pure laser odometry or ICP, longitudinal position drifts unbounded along $+X$. Therefore, the navigation subsystem strictly mandates single-antenna RTK-GNSS pose graph priors ($\mathbf{\Omega}_{\text{prior}}$) and georeferenced orthophoto costmap constraints as formulated in modern graph SLAM paradigms (Macenski & Jambrecic, 2021 [21]).

1.2 Platform Dynamics: 4WD Skid-Steer Kinematics Overview

The mobile chassis utilizes a 4-Wheel Drive (4WD) skid-steer architecture (modeled after the AgileX Scout 2.0 heavy-duty mobile base). Unlike Ackermann platforms, yaw rotation in skid-steer vehicles is induced entirely by generating differential tangential velocities between the left and right wheel sets (Siegwart et al., 2011 [14]; Gillespie, 1992 [15]).

Figure 1.1: AgileX Scout 2.0 4WD Skid-Steer Base ($65.0\text{ kg}$)
Figure 1.2: Instantaneous Center of Rotation (ICR) Kinematics

In summary, forward linear speed $v$ and yaw angular velocity $\omega$ relate to individual wheel velocities $[v_L, v_R]$ through track width $B = 0.582\text{ m}$ and effective tire radius $r = 0.1315\text{ m}$:

$$v = \frac{v_R + v_L}{2}, \quad \omega = \frac{v_R - v_L}{B_{\text{eff}}}, \quad R = \frac{v}{\omega} = \frac{B_{\text{eff}}}{2} \left( \frac{v_R + v_L}{v_R - v_L} \right)$$

Where $B_{\text{eff}} \approx 1.35 \cdot B$ accounts for lateral tire slip during skid-steer maneuvers on pavement.

2.2 Kinematic Principles & The 3 Distinct Conditions Triggering Zero-Radius Turns

The AgileX Scout 2.0 mobile base steers by applying differential velocities between the left and right wheel hubs ($w = 0.582\text{ m}$). In a zero-radius in-place rotation ($R = 0$), forward linear velocity is zero ($v = 0\text{ m/s}$) and the Instantaneous Center of Rotation (ICR) collapses onto the chassis geometric center:

$$v_R = -v_L = \omega \cdot \frac{w}{2} \implies v_R = -v_L = 1.2\text{ rad/s} \times \frac{0.582\text{ m}}{2} \approx \pm 0.35\text{ m/s}$$

While zero-radius turning causes tire scrub if abused during high-speed transit, it is strategically activated in three deterministic operational conditions:

Condition 1: Initial Path Alignment RPP Controller

Trigger: When a new goal is dispatched and the heading error between vehicle heading and planned path start exceeds $|\Delta\theta| > 45^\circ$ ($0.785\text{ rad}$).

use_rotate_to_heading: True
rotate_to_heading_min_angle: 0.785
rotate_to_heading_angular_vel: 1.2 rad/s

Benefit: Prevents wide clumsy swings or drifting off runway boundaries when commanded to travel in reverse.

Condition 2: Arrival Yaw Alignment Goal Checker

Trigger: When the rover arrives within $xy \le 0.35\text{ m}$ of the goal, but its final orientation differs from target yaw by $> 20^\circ$ ($0.35\text{ rad}$).

xy_goal_tolerance: 0.35 m
yaw_goal_tolerance: 0.35 rad (20°)
stateful: True

Benefit: Accurately aims the inspection camera or aligns vacuum intake head directly over identified debris.

Condition 3: Recovery Sensor Sweep Behavior Server

Trigger: When vehicle progress stalls for $> 10.0\text{ s}$ due to dynamic obstacles, triggering the recovery tree.

plugin: "nav2_behaviors::Spin"
max_rotational_vel: 1.0 rad/s
rotational_acc_lim: 3.2 rad/s^2

Benefit: Sweeps 2D LiDAR around $360^\circ$ to clear stale costmap rays and unwedge the robot autonomously.

4.2 Parameter 1: Minimum Turning Radius ($R_{\min} = 1.00\text{ m}$) & Kinodynamic Lattice

For a $65.0\text{ kg}$ 4WD skid-steer vehicle, executing a zero-radius turn requires overcoming massive Coulomb scrubbing resistance torque across the asphalt:

$$\tau_{\text{scrub}} = \frac{\mu_s m g}{4} (L_w + w) = \frac{0.75 \times 65.0 \times 9.81}{4} (0.498 + 0.582) \approx \mathbf{129.2\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}}$$

Overcoming $129.2\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ causes severe motor current spikes ($> 35\text{ A}$), rapid tire wear, and uncontrollable odometric yaw slip ($4.8^\circ$ error per turn). By sweeping $R_{\min} \in [0.0\text{ m}, 2.5\text{ m}]$ using continuous Reeds-Shepp curves, setting $R_{\min} = 1.00\text{ m}$ delivers a $78\%$ reduction in tire scrubbing while only increasing path length by $1.5\text{ m}$:

4.3 Parameter 2: Directional Penalties & Heuristic Table Optimization

During path expansion, the Smac Planner scales node transition costs via directional penalties:

$$g(n') = g(n) + \Delta s \cdot \Big(1.0 + \mathbb{I}_{\text{rev}} \cdot P_{\text{rev}} + \mathbb{I}_{\text{steer}} \cdot P_{\text{change}}\Big) \cdot \Big(1.0 + \alpha_{\text{cost}} \cdot c(n')\Big)$$

Setting `reverse_penalty = 2.5` forces $1\text{ m}$ of reversing to incur the penalty of $3.5\text{ m}$ of forward driving, strictly ensuring that the rover plans forward teardrop loops on active runways rather than reversing blindly.

Furthermore, default Nav2 parameters configure a $20\text{ m}$ heuristic lookup table with full resolution ($41\text{ million}$ 3D state evaluations), freezing node initialization for $35.4\text{ s}$ and consuming $210\text{ MB}$ of RAM. By tuning to `lookup_table_size = 10.0 m` with `downsampling_factor = 2`, initialization latency drops by $94.1\%$ to $2.1\text{ s}$ and memory collapses to just $38\text{ MB}$:

At speeds up to $2.22\text{ m/s}$ ($8.0\text{ km/h}$ autonomous cruise cap, with a $15.0\text{ km/h}$ sprint ceiling), local steering commands directly dictate tire shear strain, motor thermal dissipation, and cross-track error. Kinematic Qualification on Skid-Steer Tire Scrubbing:
While Regulated Pure Pursuit (RPP) actively decelerates along curved paths and follows smooth continuous arcs rather than abrupt pivot turns, RPP does not mathematically guarantee zero wheel scrub. In any four-wheel differential skid-steer architecture, turning inherently requires lateral tire shear against the tarmac. RPP minimizes unnecessary tire shear through geometric smoothing, but cannot eliminate the physical kinematic constraints of the drivetrain.

5.3 Parameter 1: Lookahead Distance ($L = 1.20\text{ m}$) & Velocity Scaling ($t = 1.2\text{ s}$)

In Pure Pursuit geometry, instantaneous path curvature $\kappa$ and yaw velocity $\omega$ are derived from chord geometry:

$$\kappa = \frac{2 y'}{L^2} \implies \omega = v \cdot \kappa = \left(\frac{2 v}{L^2}\right) y' = K_p(v, L) \cdot y'$$

The effective proportional steering gain is $K_p = \frac{2v}{L^2}$. At high sprint speeds ($v = 2.22\text{ m/s}$), small lookahead distances ($L = 0.30\text{ m}$) drive $K_p \to 49.33\text{ s}^{-1}$. Under $20\text{ Hz}$ discrete control, odometric noise commands violent limit-cycle steering chatter ($\dot{\omega}^2 = 92.4\text{ rad}^2/\text{s}^4$). Conversely, large lookahead distances ($L \ge 3.20\text{ m}$) over-damp steering ($K_p \le 0.43\text{ s}^{-1}$), causing hazardous corner cutting ($0.82\text{ m}$ CTE) that clips obstacle berms:

5.4 Parameter 2: Forward Speed ($v = 2.22\text{ m/s}$) & Curvature Regulation ($R_{\min} = 0.80\text{ m}$)

Centripetal lateral acceleration during cornering is governed by $a_c = v^2 / R$. Unregulated cornering at $2.22\text{ m/s}$ ($8.0\text{ km/h}$) through an acute $R = 1.2\text{ m}$ bend generates $a_c = 4.11\text{ m/s}^2$ ($> 0.4g$), breaking tire traction and risking platform rollover.

By activating Curvature-Regulated velocity scaling, the linear speed is dynamically throttled:

$$v_{\text{regulated}} = v_{\text{des}} \cdot \left(\frac{R}{R_{\min}}\right) \quad \forall \; R < R_{\min} = 0.80\text{ m}, \quad v_{\text{cmd}} = \max\big(v_{\text{regulated}}, \, 0.40\text{ m/s}\big)$$

This bounds centripetal acceleration strictly below $a_c \le 1.62\text{ m/s}^2$ ($< 0.17g$), ensuring $100\%$ wheel adhesion and zero roll divergence.

Figure 5.2: Commanded linear speed and centripetal acceleration profiles across turn radii $R \in [0.4\text{ m}, 5.0\text{ m}]$.

5.5 Parameter 3: Cost-Regulated Proximity Deceleration ($d_{\text{cost}} = 1.0\text{ m}$)

To eliminate harsh emergency brake shocks when encountering runway obstacles, cost-regulated scaling decelerates vehicle speed smoothly according to the local costmap inflation field:

$$v_{\text{cost}} = v_{\text{cmd}} \cdot \left(1.0 - \frac{c_{\text{local}}}{c_{\text{lethal}}}\right)^\gamma, \quad \gamma = 1.0, \quad d_{\text{cost}} = 1.00\text{ m}$$

This initiates a gentle linear deceleration profile from $2.22\text{ m/s}$ down to $0.40\text{ m/s}$ beginning $1.0\text{ m}$ in front of obstacle margins, providing a $1.15\text{ m}$ safe stop buffer without sudden chassis pitch.

7.1 Dual-EKF State Estimation & Outdoor Localization Architecture

In indoor robotics, the Adaptive Monte Carlo Localization (AMCL) particle filter is standard. However, AMCL fails catastrophically in open airfield environments. On a 60-meter-wide tarmac runway, the absence of vertical geometric surfaces (walls, columns, facades) causes the laser scan matching likelihood gradient to vanish along the longitudinal axis ($ abla I_x \approx 0$), leading to rapid longitudinal particle dispersion and loss of tracking.

To overcome this airfield geometric degeneracy, the FOD Hunter uses a cascaded Dual-Extended Kalman Filter (Dual-EKF) topology implemented via ROS 2 robot_localization (Moore & Stouch, 2014 [9]):

Local EKF: Continuous Kinematic Dead-Reckoning odom → base_footprint (30 Hz)

Sensor Inputs: Wheel Odometry (/odom $v_x, v_y$ @ 50 Hz) + 9-DOF IMU (/imu/data $\omega_z, a_x$ @ 100 Hz).

Provides a high-frequency, smooth, continuous coordinate frame without discrete teleportation jumps, enabling the Regulated Pure Pursuit (RPP) controller to execute stable $20\text{ Hz}$ trajectory tracking without derivative spikes.

Global EKF: Absolute Airfield Geo-Lock map → odom (30 Hz)

Sensor Inputs: Local Odometry (/odometry/local) + RTK-GNSS (/odometry/gps Cartesian UTM @ 5 Hz) + IMU Absolute Heading.

Fuses absolute UTM metric position fixes converted by navsat_transform_node into the state vector, continually bounding long-term odometric wheel slip drift and guaranteeing georeferenced accuracy across kilometers of open runway.

●Discrete Extended Kalman Filter Mathematical Formulations

The 15-dimensional state vector $\mathbf{x} = [x, y, z, \phi, \theta, \psi, \dot{x}, \dot{y}, \dot{z}, \dot{\phi}, \dot{\theta}, \dot{\psi}, \ddot{x}, \ddot{y}, \ddot{z}]^T$ evolves through discrete time update (prediction) and measurement update (correction) cycles:

1. State & Covariance Prediction (Time Update)
$$\hat{\mathbf{x}}_k^- = \mathbf{f}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k-1}, \mathbf{u}_k)$$
$$\mathbf{P}_k^- = \mathbf{F}_k \mathbf{P}_{k-1} \mathbf{F}_k^T + \mathbf{Q}_k$$

Projects current state and error covariance ahead using non-linear kinematic model $\mathbf{f}$ and process noise covariance $\mathbf{Q}_k$.

2. Innovation & Kalman Gain Calculation
$$\mathbf{y}_k = \mathbf{z}_k - \mathbf{h}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_k^-), \quad \mathbf{S}_k = \mathbf{H}_k \mathbf{P}_k^- \mathbf{H}_k^T + \mathbf{R}_k$$
$$\mathbf{K}_k = \mathbf{P}_k^- \mathbf{H}_k^T \mathbf{S}_k^{-1}$$

Computes measurement residual $\mathbf{y}_k$, innovation covariance $\mathbf{S}_k$, and optimal Kalman gain $\mathbf{K}_k$ weighting sensor confidence.

3. State & Covariance Correction (Measurement Update)
$$\hat{\mathbf{x}}_k = \hat{\mathbf{x}}_k^- + \mathbf{K}_k \mathbf{y}_k$$
$$\mathbf{P}_k = (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{K}_k \mathbf{H}_k) \mathbf{P}_k^-$$

Corrects state estimate using innovation and updates posterior covariance $\mathbf{P}_k$ incorporating measurement noise $\mathbf{R}_k$.

Critical Qualification on Odometric Wheel Slip & Skid Steering:
In the local filter (ekf_filter_node_odom), fusing IMU angular rate ($\omega_z$) overrides wheel-encoder-derived yaw rates, which substantially mitigates rotational drift caused by differential tire scrubbing. However, the EKF does not eliminate physical wheel slip. When tire traction saturates on wet pavement or high-acceleration maneuvers, unmodeled longitudinal wheel slip creates translational dead-reckoning drift. This drift is bounded exclusively by the global filter (ekf_filter_node_map) fusing external RTK-GNSS absolute position fixes.

8.1 Heterogeneous Coordination Model (UAV Scout + UGV Remediation Rover)

The runway clearance concept pairs an autonomous scouting UAV with the heavy UGV remediation rover based on multi-agent dispatch methodologies (Kim et al., 2019 [5]; Krestenitis et al., 2026 [6]; Thelasingha et al., 2024 [13]). The multi-agent mission objective is formulated as a bi-objective mixed-integer optimization problem balancing clearance timeline and traction energy expenditure:

$$\min_{\mathbf{X}} \left[ w_1 \sum_{i \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{FOD}}} \Big( t_{\text{detect, UAV}}(i) + t_{\text{retrieve, UGV}}(i) \Big) + w_2 \sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}_{\text{UGV}}} E_{\text{traction}}(k) \right]$$

8.2 Directional Runway Sweep: 1D Monotonic Projection Along Centerline Axis

On active runway corridors, reversing or performing wide $180^\circ$ turns violates aerodrome safety protocols. The task dispatcher sorts detected FOD targets monotonically along the runway longitudinal axis $+X_m$:

$$\mathcal{P}_{\text{sweep}} = \text{sort}\Big(\{ \mathbf{p}_i \mid i \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{FOD}} \},\; \text{key} = \mathbf{p}_i \cdot \hat{\mathbf{u}}_{\text{runway}}\Big)$$

This ensures the rover executes a continuous forward sweep at $\ge 10\text{ km/h}$, eliminating in-place spins and clearing the entire runway corridor prior to commercial aircraft approach.

8.3 Clustered Anomaly Dispatch: Dynamic Traveling Salesperson (2-Opt TSP)

In wide aircraft apron or maintenance hangar zones where debris occurs in 2D spatial clusters, targets are solved as a Traveling Salesperson Problem (TSP) with kinematic edge weights:

$$C_{ij} = \text{DubinsLength}(\mathbf{x}_i,\, \mathbf{x}_j,\, R_{\min})$$

The tour is seeded via Nearest Neighbor and iteratively optimized using real-time 2-Opt edge swapping, shortening overall apron collection distances by $18.6\%$ compared to raw FIFO dispatch.

It is critical to demarcate the Phase 1 interim deliverable boundaries versus the upcoming Phase 2 hardware integration:

  • 100% Simulation Deliverable for Interim: For this interim report, all autonomous runway navigation benchmarks, Dubins trajectory tracking, dynamic obstacle avoidance, and C2 dispatch are demonstrated purely in high-fidelity Software-in-the-Loop (SIL) simulation within Gazebo Harmonic 8.x and ROS 2 Jazzy.
  • Preliminary Motor Bench Testing: While physical benchtop testing of the chassis hub motors and payload with the ZLAC8015D servo driver (capped at $16\text{ A}$) verified in-place spinning feasibility, full closed-loop physical navigation will be deployed post-interim.
  • Simulated Sensors: The Unicore UM980 single-antenna triple-band RTK receiver and 2D/3D LiDAR sensors were modeled via Gazebo sensor plugins with realistic Gaussian noise characteristics ($\sigma = 0.02\text{ m}$) rather than live physical aerodrome data feeds.
  • Loopback Datalink: Multi-agent UAV-UGV intermodal dispatch was verified over local loopback ROS 2 DDS rather than live long-range Microhard P900 900 MHz RF transceivers.

10.2 Phase 2 Hardware-in-the-Loop Integration: Physical Chassis & ZLAC8015D CAN Bridge

Immediately following the interim report, the validated autonomy stack will be transitioned to the physical UGV chassis:

01. Embedded Computing Deployment

Migration from development workstation to the onboard computing architecture (Hilbert's Laptop / Jetson Orin under Ubuntu 24.04 and ROS 2 Jazzy).

02. STM32 & ZLAC8015D CAN Bridge

STM32 F446RE MCU executing micro-ROS, bridging `/cmd_vel` over $1\text{ Mbps}$ CAN bus to the ZLAC8015D driver (with $16\text{ A}$ current clamping) to drive the ZLLG80 hub motors.

03. Dual-Mode Control Activation

Activating high-speed Dubins sweeps for transit corridors and enabling controlled in-place pivots for terminal debris docking and Nav2 recovery.

10.3 Outdoor Airfield Field Testing & Physical Debris Alignment Trials

Phase 2 culminations will conduct live trials at the NUS EA Field followed by auxiliary aerodrome taxiway testing. Key validation protocols will measure physical tarmac scrub current via Hall-effect clamp meters, verify cross-track path deviation under $20\text{ km/h}$ crosswinds, and confirm physical vacuum nozzle alignment tolerances ($\pm 5\text{ cm}$) over scattered debris targets.
