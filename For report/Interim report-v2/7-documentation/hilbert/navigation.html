SECTION 1.0

Global Path Planning: SmacPlannerHybrid & Non-Holonomic $SE(2)$ Kinematics

SmacPlannerHybrid (Dubins)

1.1 Physical Problem Statement: Tire Scrubbing & High-Current Motor Stalls on Asphalt

The physical UGV utilizes a 4WD skid-steer chassis with 8-inch direct-drive hub motors. On high-friction airfield asphalt ($\mu \approx 0.8$), executing standard zero-radius point turns (or sharp turns generated by holonomic grid planners like NavFn / 2D A*) requires lateral tire slippage against the ground. The resistive breakaway scrubbing torque for each wheel is governed by:

$$\tau_{\text{skid, per motor}} = \frac{m \cdot g \cdot \mu \cdot r}{4 \cdot \eta_{\text{gear}}} = \frac{65.0 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.80 \cdot 0.1315}{4 \cdot 0.85} \approx 19.73\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$
High-Current Bus Collapse Hazard: With a motor torque constant $K_t \approx 1.057\text{ N}\cdot\text{m/A}$, drawing $19.73\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ of static breakaway scrub torque pulls $I = \tau / K_t \approx 18.66\text{ A}$ per hub motor. Across 4 hub motors, an in-place pivot turn demands $>74.6\text{ A}$ total instantaneous bus current ($>3.58\text{ kW}$ at 48V). This causes severe DC bus voltage droop, tripping the BMS overcurrent protection circuit, stalling motors, and burning rubber on high-friction runway asphalt. This physically justifies strictly enforcing a continuous minimum turning radius ($R_{\min} \ge 1.50\text{ m}$).

1.2 Mathematical Solution: Non-Holonomic Dubins Motion Model ($R_{\min} \ge 1.50\text{ m}$)

To eliminate static scrubbing, the global planner must strictly constrain all trajectory curves to kinematically executable continuous arc segments with curvature $\kappa \le 1 / R_{\min}$. The vehicle state space is modeled in $SE(2) = \mathbb{R}^2 \times SO(2)$ as $\mathbf{x} = [x, y, \theta]^T$:

$$\begin{bmatrix} \dot{x} \\ \dot{y} \\ \dot{\theta} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} v \cos \theta \\ v \sin \theta \\ \frac{v}{R} \end{bmatrix}, \quad |R| \ge R_{\min} = 1.50\text{ m}$$

SmacPlannerHybrid (Macenski et al., 2020) solves this through a Hybrid-A* search architecture. It expands discrete 3D search nodes $(x, y, \theta)$ with continuous Dubins motion primitives, guided by an admissible, non-overestimating dual heuristic $f(\mathbf{x})$:

$$f(\mathbf{x}) = g(\mathbf{x}) + \max\Big(h_{\text{Dubins}}(\mathbf{x}),\, h_{\text{grid}}(\mathbf{x})\Big) + C_{\text{penalty}}$$
  • $g(\mathbf{x})$: Exact path cost accumulated from the start node, including steering change penalties.
  • $h_{\text{Dubins}}(\mathbf{x})$: Analytic Dubins path distance accounting for non-holonomic orientation alignment with terminal pose.
  • $h_{\text{grid}}(\mathbf{x})$: 2D Dijkstra obstacle distance transform ignoring orientation constraints (prevents search from getting trapped in local costmap minima).
  • $C_{\text{penalty}}$: Exponential obstacle proximity cost and non-straight steering angle penalty ($1.30\times$).

SECTION 2.0

Local Trajectory Tracking: Regulated Pure Pursuit (RPP) & MPPI

Deterministic & Stochastic Control

At speeds reaching $\ge 10\text{ km/h}$ ($2.78\text{ m/s}$), standard path trackers (e.g., DWB / generic TEB) suffer from severe lateral hunting and latency spikes. Two complementary local controllers were evaluated and configured within the Nav2 framework (Macenski et al., 2020, 2022):

Primary Controller: Regulated Pure Pursuit (RPP)

50 Hz • 1 CPU Core

Geometric lookahead tracker with adaptive velocity regulation (Macenski et al., 2020). The instantaneous path curvature $\kappa = \frac{1}{R}$ and commanded yaw rate $\omega$ are derived from the lookahead point $(x_L, y_L)$ in the vehicle local frame with lookahead distance $L$:

$$\kappa = \frac{2 y_L}{L^2} \implies \omega = v \cdot \kappa = \frac{2 v y_L}{L^2}$$

Linear velocity dynamically regulates as a function of path curvature and acceleration limits:

$$v = \min\left(v_{\text{desired}},\, \frac{v_{\text{desired}}}{\kappa \cdot R_{\min}},\, v_{\text{accel\_limit}}\right)$$
Key Configuration Decision: use_rotate_to_heading: false is strictly enforced. When a new waypoint is received, the UGV smoothly accelerates forward along a tangent arc rather than executing a high-current in-place turn.

Secondary Controller: MPPI (Stochastic Optimal)

2048 CUDA Cores

Model Predictive Path Integral controller for complex obstacle fields. Evaluates $K = 2048$ parallel randomized trajectory rollouts $\mathbf{u}_{t, k} = \mathbf{v}_t + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{t, k}$ over a 3.0-second time horizon:

$$\mathbf{u}_t^* = \sum_{k=1}^K w_k \mathbf{u}_{t, k}, \quad w_k = \frac{\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\lambda} S(\mathbf{u}_k)\right)}{\sum_{j=1}^K \exp\left(-\frac{1}{\lambda} S(\mathbf{u}_j)\right)}$$

Where $S(\mathbf{u}_k)$ evaluates path adherence, obstacle collision costs, and kinematic acceleration limits.

Compute Trade-Off: RPP executes deterministically on the onboard CPU with <1.8 ms latency per cycle, making it the primary controller for Phase 1. MPPI is reserved for high-density FOD clutter modes on the Jetson Orin.
SECTION 3.0

Sensor Fusion & State Estimation: Dual-EKF Architecture

robot_localization

Airfield runways represent open, feature-sparse outdoor expanses where traditional indoor localization algorithms (such as Particle Filter AMCL) fail due to the lack of vertical geometric reflectors. A dual-tier Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) is deployed via robot_localization (Moore & Stouch, 2014):

Level 1: Local Continuous EKF (odom → base_link)

  • Rate: 50 Hz continuous execution.
  • Wheel Odometry: Fuses linear velocity ($v_x$) from optical motor encoders.
  • 9-DOF IMU (100 Hz): Fuses angular yaw rate ($\dot{\theta} = \omega_z$) and longitudinal acceleration ($a_x$).
  • Skid-Steer Rejection: Because wheel odometry slips heavily during turns, the EKF ignores wheel yaw rate and relies entirely on the calibrated IMU gyroscope for heading change.

Level 2: Global Map EKF (map → odom)

  • Rate: 10 Hz global correction.
  • RTK-GNSS Receiver: Unicore UM980 dual-antenna receiver providing centimeter-level absolute positioning ($\pm 2.0\text{ cm}$) and true geographic heading.
  • Drift Elimination: Continuously estimates and offsets the accumulated odometric drift without causing sudden trajectory discontinuities.

3.1 Geodetic Coordinate Frame Transformation (WGS84 → UTM → Map Frame)

When an aerial UAV scout detects a piece of FOD (Shan et al., 2025; Krestenitis et al., 2026), it transmits the target location as WGS84 geodetic coordinates $(\phi, \lambda, h)$. The UGV's navsat_transform_node converts the coordinates into UTM Zone 48N metric Cartesian coordinates $(X_{\text{utm}}, Y_{\text{utm}})$, which are then transformed into the local Nav2 map frame via a 2D affine rotation matrix:

$$\begin{bmatrix} X_m \\ Y_m \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos \theta_0 & \sin \theta_0 \\ -\sin \theta_0 & \cos \theta_0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} X_{\text{utm}} - X_{\text{datum}} \\ Y_{\text{utm}} - Y_{\text{datum}} \end{bmatrix}$$

Where $(X_{\text{datum}}, Y_{\text{datum}})$ represents the surveyed runway threshold origin, and $\theta_0$ aligns the $+X_m$ axis directly along the runway magnetic centerline heading.

SECTION 4.0

Multi-Agent Intermodal Dispatch & Runway Sweep Optimization

Intermodal Multi-Agent Dispatch

In accordance with heterogeneous autonomous fleet coordination models (Multimodal Transportation, Elsevier 2025; Kim et al., 2019), the runway inspection workflow is formulated as an intermodal, two-tier hierarchical dispatch problem designed to minimize the critical operational metric: Total Runway Closure Time ($T_{\text{closure}}$).

Intermodal Coordination Paradigm (UAV + UGV): The high-speed aerial scout (UAV) conducts continuous rapid orthomosaic scanning at $\ge 40\text{ km/h}$, queueing georeferenced FOD candidates into a centralized C2 dispatch matrix. Ground sweepers (UGVs) are dispatched dynamically using an optimized cost objective: $$\min \ J = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{FOD}}} \left( \alpha \cdot T_{\text{travel}}(i) + \beta \cdot T_{\text{collection}}(i) \right) + \gamma \cdot T_{\text{clearance}}$$ where $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ are operational priority weights penalizing runway occupancy duration.

1. Directional Runway Sweep (1D Monotonic Projection)

For debris distributed along an active runway strip, targets are sorted monotonically along the primary runway longitudinal axis $+X_m$. This guarantees the UGV sweeps in a single forward pass at $\ge 10\text{ km/h}$, eliminating wasteful $180^\circ$ turnaround loops on the runway centerline.

2. Dynamic TSP (Nearest Neighbor + 2-Opt Optimization)

For localized apron and maintenance bay clusters, the scheduler solves the Traveling Salesperson Problem (TSP) using Euclidean distance matrices, with 2-Opt iterative improvements executed in <5 ms to minimize total mission time and battery expenditure.
