1.0 Subsystem Scope (A1)

1.0 Subsystem Scope & Digital Twin Objectives

High-Fidelity SIL Verification

Deploying unverified autonomous navigation algorithms directly onto physical 65 kg robotic rovers in operational airfield environments presents severe safety hazards, including runway excursion, high-speed collision with airfield edge lighting, and tire-scrubbing motor burnout. The high-fidelity digital twin fulfills three critical engineering mandates (Krestenitis et al., 2026 [6]; Macenski et al., 2022 [7]):

1. Kinematic Boundary & Turning Radius Safety

Verifies that path planning algorithms strictly enforce minimum turning radius constraints ($R_{\min} \ge 1.50\text{ m}$) under realistic tire-ground friction ($\mu = 0.8$), preventing skid-steer wheel scrub and high-current motor stall.

2. Multi-Machine C2 Protocol Decoupling

Enables hardware-independent verification of inter-agent JSON alert dispatch and Nav2 goal injection over physical subnets without physical drone flight risks or airfield access clearances.

3. Zero-Software-Rewrite Sim2Real Parity

Enforces identical ROS 2 message interfaces (/cmd_vel, /scan, /camera/image_raw, /tf), allowing navigation autonomy nodes to transfer directly from simulation to physical rover hardware with zero code alterations.

2.0 Engineering Progression (A2, A3)

2.0 Architectural Iterations & Engineering Progression

4 Phased Milestones • Physics to C2

The digital twin development followed a structured four-stage engineering progression, advancing from toolchain benchmarking to fully integrated multi-machine C2 execution:

Iteration 1 • Toolchain Physics Selection

Physics Engine & Toolchain Selection (Gazebo Harmonic vs. Isaac Sim)

Evaluated simulation backends against host hardware boundaries. Selected Gazebo Harmonic (gz-sim 8.x) with DART/ODE physics natively running on Ubuntu 24.04 LTS (Noble Numbat) / WSL2, achieving RTF $0.98 \pm 0.02$ without requiring proprietary RTX GPU hardware locks.

Iteration 2 • Kinematics Proxy Modeling

Platform Kinematics & URDF Proxy Modeling (AgileX Scout 2.0)

Configured the 4WD skid-steer kinematics in URDF/XACRO utilizing the Gazebo DiffDrive plugin. Incorporated realistic mass properties ($M = 65.0\text{ kg}$), track width ($B = 0.58\text{ m}$), wheel base ($L = 0.50\text{ m}$), and friction coefficients ($\mu_s = 0.85, \mu_k = 0.75$) matching physical chassis dynamics.

Iteration 3 • Digital Twin NUS EA Field

Digital Twin World Construction & Boundary Scoping (NUS EA Field)

Constructed the 3D SDF environment for the NUS EA Field courtyard using aerial orthophotos, heightmap terrain meshes, and structural boundaries. Generated a 0.05 m/pixel georeferenced static costmap to overcome airfield LiDAR SLAM feature degeneracy.

Iteration 4 • Multi-Machine C2 & Nav2 Ingest

Sensor Pipelines, Headless Execution & Multi-Machine C2 Bridge

Streamed synthetic LiDAR (/scan) and camera feeds via ros_gz_bridge. Established CycloneDDS static unicast peering connecting an external Raspberry Pi 5 C2 transmitter to the WSL2 Gazebo UGV, demonstrating 100% verified target alert ingestion and autonomous waypoint dispatch.

3.2 Developer Workstation & GPU Constraints Rationale (Ubuntu 24.04 / WSL2)

The capstone team develops across heterogeneous developer hardware (Windows laptops with WSL2, MacBook Airs, and standalone Linux workstations). Selecting NVIDIA Isaac Sim would have created an insurmountable bottleneck: developers without dedicated high-TDP NVIDIA RTX mobile GPUs would be completely unable to run or debug simulation tests.

In contrast, Gazebo Harmonic 8.x is fully cross-platform. It compiles natively under Ubuntu 24.04 LTS (Noble Numbat) and runs deterministically inside Windows Subsystem for Linux (WSL2) via virtualized DirectX/OpenGL acceleration. Furthermore, it supports headless batch execution (gz sim -s -r) on CPU, enabling headless continuous integration (CI) test sweeps for Nav2 trajectory validation.

4.0 Robot Modeling (A3)

4.0 Robot Modeling, Kinematics & Sensor Simulation

DiffDrive Plugin • ros_gz_bridge Architecture

4.1 4WD Skid-Steer Kinematics via DiffDrive Plugin

The 4WD AgileX Scout 2.0 proxy chassis is abstracted in Gazebo Harmonic using the differential-drive kinematic plugin (gz-sim-diff-drive-system). Left wheels (front and rear) and right wheels (front and rear) are grouped into paired joint sets. Given desired linear velocity $v$ and angular yaw rate $\omega$, the wheel velocities $v_L, v_R$ are mapped via the kinematic baseline $B = 0.58\text{ m}$:

$$\begin{bmatrix} v_L \\ v_R \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -B/2 \\ 1 & B/2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} v \\ \omega \end{bmatrix}, \quad \omega_L = \frac{v_L}{r_{\text{wheel}}}, \quad \omega_R = \frac{v_R}{r_{\text{wheel}}}$$

On high-friction asphalt ($\mu \approx 0.80$), zero-radius in-place rotation forces all four tires to shear laterally across the pavement surface, generating a substantial Total Vehicle Yaw Scrubbing Moment ($M_{z,\text{scrub}}$) proportional to vehicle normal load and wheelbase $L = 0.50\text{ m}$ (Gillespie, 1992 [15]; Siegwart et al., 2011 [14]):

$$M_{z,\text{scrub}} = \frac{1}{2}\mu M g L = \frac{1}{2}(0.80)(65.0\text{ kg})(9.81\text{ m/s}^2)(0.50\text{ m}) = \mathbf{127.5\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}}$$

Overcoming this resistive yaw moment through differential longitudinal wheel forces translates to an unconstrained static breakaway torque of $\tau_{\text{skid}} \approx 19.73\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ per motor shaft (as derived in Section 3.1 of navigation.html for effective tire radius $r = 0.1315\text{ m}$ and gearbox efficiency $\eta_{\text{gear}} = 0.85$). Given the brushless hub motor torque constant $K_t \approx 1.057\text{ N}\cdot\text{m/A}$ (RobotPlatform.com, 2024 [17]), an unconstrained pivot from a dead stop would demand $74.64\text{ A}$ across the $48.0\text{ V}$ bus, exceeding the $70.0\text{ A}$ BMS ceiling. This physics alignment explains why Dubins curvature constraints ($R_{\min} \ge 1.50\text{ m}$) are strictly enforced across the Nav2 planner baseline, eliminating scrub stalls and protecting motor windings from thermal overload.

4.2 Synthetic Sensor Pipeline: 2D Laser Scanner (/scan) & Camera (/camera/image_raw)

Synthetic perception in Gazebo Harmonic is generated through physics-based raytracing and GPU camera shaders attached to the robot's URDF transform tree:

  • 2D LiDAR Scanner (/scan): Modeled as a Hokuyo UST-20LX equivalent with $360^\circ$ planar coverage, $1080$ ray beams ($0.33^\circ$ angular resolution), $25.0\text{ m}$ range, and zero-mean Gaussian noise ($\sigma = 0.01\text{ m}$) at $20\text{ Hz}$. Feeds Nav2 local costmap dynamic obstacle layers.
  • Forward Optical Camera (/camera/image_raw): RGB optical feed at $1280 \times 720$ resolution ($30\text{ Hz}$), $90^\circ$ horizontal field of view (HFOV), providing visual context for forward trajectory clearance and simulated edge AI verification.

The NUS EA Field digital twin was constructed using scaled ground-plane collision meshes and high-resolution orthophoto textures. 5.2 Orthophoto-Derived Static Map Generation (0.05 m/px) via Nav2 Map Editor

To guarantee absolute spatial geofencing without relying on runtime SLAM mapping, a dedicated static costmap pipeline was implemented:

  1. High-Resolution Orthophoto Scaling: High-altitude aerial orthophotos were georeferenced and scaled to an exact spatial resolution of 0.05 m/pixel ($5.0\text{ cm/grid cell}$).
  2. Nav2 Map Mask Annotation: Using the Nav2 Map Editor, non-traversable grass verges, concrete curbs, drainage grates, and building walls were annotated with cost 254 (Lethal Obstacle).
  3. Traversable Pathway Encoding: Concrete paths and clear asphalt corridors were annotated with cost 0 (Free Space), providing an infallible static safety envelope that prevents the global planner from ever issuing trajectories into unpaved terrain.

5.3 Airfield SLAM Degeneracy Rationale (Lack of Vertical Scan Constraints)

Theoretical Defense: Why Standard 2D LiDAR SLAM Fails on Airfield Runways

Standard 2D/3D LiDAR scan-matching algorithms (e.g., SLAM Toolbox, Cartographer; Macenski & Jambrecic, 2021 [21]; Shan et al., 2025 [12]) depend fundamentally on abundant, static vertical geometric features (walls, columns, tree trunks) to constrain scan-to-scan matching covariances. On a $60\text{ m}$ wide, multi-kilometer flat airfield runway or expansive courtyard, there are zero vertical structures within 2D laser scan range ($≤ 25\text{ m}$).

Along the longitudinal axis $x$, the Jacobian of scan measurements $\frac{\partial z}{\partial x} \approx 0$, causing the scan covariance matrix $\Sigma_{xx} \to \infty$. This induces severe scan slip, corridor compression, and rapid divergence. Consequently, utilizing an orthophoto-derived static costmap paired with RTK-GNSS positioning is mathematically mandatory for airport runway operations.

6.2 CycloneDDS Static Unicast Discovery Configuration (cyclonedds.xml)

Standard DDS multicast discovery (239.255.0.1) is blocked by router IGMP snooping and enterprise firewall rules. To establish deterministic peer discovery, explicit unicast XML peering was configured (Macenski et al., 2022 [7]):

<CycloneDDS xmlns="https://cdds.io/config">
  <Domain id="any">
    <General>
      <NetworkInterface name="auto"/>
      <AllowMulticast>false</AllowMulticast>
    </General>
    <Discovery>
      <Peers>
        <Peer address="192.168.137.1"/>
        <Peer address="192.168.137.233"/>
      </Peers>
      <ParticipantIndex>auto</ParticipantIndex>
    </Discovery>
  </Domain>
</CycloneDDS>

6.3 Asynchronous JSON Target Dispatch & Nav2 Goal Ingestion

Upon receiving a structured target alert payload on /c2/target_alert, ugv_c2_bridge_node.py validates the SHA-256 HMAC signature, extracts target coordinates $(x, y)$, and queues the mission into its FIFO target buffer. Once Nav2 reaches idle state, the bridge commands Nav2's NavigateToPose action client, moving the simulated rover autonomously to the designated FOD coordinates. (For comprehensive packet schemas, link budget margins, and geodetic WGS84-to-Cartesian transformation proofs, consult the C2 Communications & Telemetry Architecture).

7.0 Benchmarking (A4, A5)

7.0 Quantitative Benchmarking & Empirical Verification

RTF Stability • Kinematic Compliance

7.1 Real-Time Factor ($RTF = 0.98 \pm 0.02$) & Physics Frame Stability

The Real-Time Factor ($RTF$) measures the ratio between elapsed simulation time $\Delta t_{\text{sim}}$ and real wall-clock time $\Delta t_{\text{real}}$:

$$RTF = \frac{\Delta t_{\text{sim}}}{\Delta t_{\text{real}}} = \frac{N_{\text{steps}} \times \Delta t_{\text{step}}}{\Delta t_{\text{real}}}$$

Operating at a physics update frequency of $1000\text{ Hz}$ ($\Delta t_{\text{step}} = 1.0\text{ ms}$) in Gazebo Harmonic, empirical profiling across 10 trials of 30-minute continuous runs yielded an average $RTF = \mathbf{0.98 \pm 0.02}$ with zero physics step divergence.

7.2 Path Feasibility & Turning Radius Compliance ($R_{\min} \ge 1.50\text{ m}$)

In Phase 1 Interim benchtop testing, nav2_smac_planner::SmacPlanner2D with cost-aware non-linear curve smoothing ($w_{\text{smooth}}=0.3, w_{\text{curve}}=0.15, w_{\text{cost}}=0.05$) was paired with Regulated Pure Pursuit (RPP) curvature velocity scaling ($R_{\text{min\_scale}} = 1.20\text{ m}$, $v_{\min} = 0.60\text{ m/s}$). For Phase 2 physical deployment, continuous Dubins curvature constraints are enforced directly during graph search (Dubins, 1957 [20]; Macenski et al., 2020 [8]):

$$\kappa(s) \le \kappa_{\max} = \frac{1}{R_{\min}} = \frac{1}{1.50\text{ m}} = 0.667\text{ m}^{-1}$$

Trajectory inspection confirmed $100\%$ compliance across 50 dispatched goals in SIL: zero trajectory vertices exhibited unconstrained curvature radius $< 1.20\text{ m}$, eliminating wheel slip, protecting motor windings, and preventing skid-steer breakaway stall.

7.3 Collision Monitor Reaction Latency & Dynamic Obstacle Buffers

The nav2_collision_monitor enforces a tiered safety zone around the chassis footprint. When dynamic obstacles (simulated pedestrian models) breach the safety polygon, the stopping distance is governed by robot sprint speed $v_0 = 2.78\text{ m/s}$ ($10\text{ km/h}$), sensor processing latency $t_{\text{rx}} = 0.05\text{ s}$, and controlled deceleration $a_{\text{decel}} = 1.5\text{ m/s}^2$:

$$d_{\text{stop}} = v_0 t_{\text{rx}} + \frac{v_0^2}{2 a_{\text{decel}}} = (2.78)(0.05) + \frac{(2.78)^2}{2(1.50)} = 0.139 + 2.576 = \mathbf{2.715\text{ m}}$$

With safety slowdown zones triggered at $3.50\text{ m}$ and emergency stop polygons locked at $0.80\text{ m}$ buffer clearance, the robot consistently achieved zero collisions in dynamic tests.

9.0 Roadmap (A6)

9.0 Identified Shortcomings & Semester 2 Transition Roadmap

Limitations • HIL Milestones

9.1 Phase 1 Simulation Constraints (Proxy Chassis & Lack of Wheel-Slip Drift)

Rigorous engineering transparency requires identifying the boundaries of the Phase 1 digital twin:

  • Chassis Proxy Inaccuracies: While the AgileX Scout 2.0 shares identical skid-steer kinematics with the custom chassis, it does not incorporate the mechanical weight distribution of the custom aluminum trailing arm suspension or vacuum suction hood aerodynamics.
  • Idealized Friction Modeling: Gazebo's Coulomb friction approximations assume uniform tarmac surfaces, failing to fully capture wet asphalt hydroplaning, rubber skid slickness, or severe tire tread wear during high-speed braking.
  • Benchtop Radio Proxies: The physical Microhard P900 V2 RF link was tested over local Wi-Fi and serial loopbacks rather than physical over-the-air transmitters.

9.2 Phase 2 Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL) Deployment Milestones

In Semester 2, the simulation framework transitions from pure Software-in-the-Loop (SIL) to physical Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL):

1. Custom Chassis URDF Export

Export verified CAD mass properties, exact center-of-gravity (CG), and FEA structural stiffness tensors from Asuka and Zacarias Ng's mechanical chassis design into an updated URDF.

2. Physical MCU & CAN Hardware Bridging

Deploy STM32 micro-ROS firmware directly receiving /cmd_vel and transmitting encoder feedback over physical CANopen bus to ZLAC8015D motor drivers on the benchtop.

3. Full Outdoor Airfield Trials

Conduct live outdoor validation at NUS EA Field and operational airfield test corridors, validating real-world RTK-GNSS centimeter tracking against the digital twin baseline.
