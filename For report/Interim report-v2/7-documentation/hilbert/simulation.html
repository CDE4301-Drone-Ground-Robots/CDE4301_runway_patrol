Core Rationale • Digital Twin Mandate

Purpose of Hunter Simulation

High-Fidelity SIL Verification

Deploying unverified autonomous navigation algorithms directly onto physical 65 kg robotic rovers in operational airfield environments presents severe safety hazards, including runway excursion, high-speed collision with airfield edge lighting, and tire-scrubbing motor burnout. The high-fidelity digital twin fulfills four essential engineering purposes:

1. Risk-Free Kinematic Testing

Verifies vehicle kinematic behavior, dynamic stability, and turning dynamics under realistic tire-ground friction ($\mu = 0.8$), preventing skid-steer wheel scrub and high-current motor stall without risking physical equipment damage.

2. Rapid Navigation Tuning Iterations

Enables automated testing across hundreds of waypoint dispatches, dynamic obstacle positions, and randomized lighting conditions at accelerated physics stepping rates ($1000\text{ Hz}$) with zero battery recharge downtime.

3. Simulate C2 Communication Protocol

Validates inter-agent JSON mission alert dispatch and Nav2 goal injection over physical CycloneDDS subnets (connecting an external Raspberry Pi 5 to Hunter) without physical drone flight risks or airfield access clearances.

4. Zero-Software-Rewrite Sim2Real Parity

Enforces identical ROS 2 message interfaces (/cmd_vel, /scan, /camera/image_raw, /tf), allowing navigation autonomy nodes to transfer directly from simulation to physical Hunter hardware with zero code alterations.

The simulation was conducted across two distinct high-fidelity environments, reflecting an engineering progression shaped by operational constraints and deployment realities:

Stage 1 • Robot Model

1.0 Importing a Model: AgileX Scout 2.0 & CAD-to-URDF Pipeline

Kinematics • TF Tree

1.1 Why AgileX Scout 2.0?

Developing an autonomous navigation stack requires a verified kinematic and dynamic platform before custom chassis machining and PCB assembly are complete. The commercial AgileX Scout 2.0 4WD mobile robot was selected as the digital twin proxy because it closely replicates the target physical Hunter across core parameters:

  • Kinematic Steering: 4WD skid-steer differential drive actuation sharing identical velocity mapping matrices.
  • Payload Class & Mass: $65.0\text{ kg}$ total vehicle mass with a low center of gravity (CoG) matching the heavy battery and vacuum payload.
  • Electrical Drivetrain: Independent 48V brushless hub motors with high-torque reduction gearboxes ($r_{\text{wheel}} = 0.165\text{ m}$).
AgileX Scout 2.0: Verified 4WD Skid-Steer Baseline Model
Kinematic Design Principle: Wheelbase-to-Trackwidth Ratio ($L/B$) in Differential Drive

In 4WD skid-steer and differential drive platforms, turning requires tires to slip laterally against the ground. The ratio of wheelbase $L$ (front-to-rear axle distance) to trackwidth $B$ (left-to-right wheel separation) is a critical governing parameter:

$$\text{Ratio} = \frac{L}{B} = \frac{0.498\text{ m}}{0.582\text{ m}} \approx \mathbf{0.855} < 1.0$$

If $\frac{L}{B} > 1.0$ (a long and narrow chassis), the resistive lateral scrubbing moment dominates over the tractive steering leverage of the tires, causing excessive wheel scrub, extreme motor current draw, violent shaking, and potential motor stall during turns. Maintaining a ratio of $\frac{L}{B} < 1.0$ (ideally between $0.7$ and $0.9$) provides sufficient trackwidth leverage to overcome tire-road scrubbing friction, ensuring responsive and agile yaw rotation without exceeding motor current ceilings.

1.2 Steps Taken in Model Importation

The model ingestion pipeline transitioned from mechanical CAD design to live ROS 2 simulation across three distinct engineering steps:

Step 1

SolidWorks CAD to URDF

The Unified Robot Description Format (URDF) is an XML schema defining the kinematic and dynamic properties of the robot:

  • Visual vs. Collision Meshes: High-polygon STLs are assigned to visual tags for photorealistic rendering, while collision meshes are simplified to lightweight bounding boxes and cylinders. Simplified collision shapes eliminate solver computational bottlenecks, ensuring a 1000 Hz physics stepping rate.
  • Mass & 3D Inertia Tensors: Masses, centers of mass, and 3D inertia matrices ($I_{xx}, I_{yy}, I_{zz}$) were calculated directly from CAD material assignments to ensure realistic momentum, braking distance, and roll/pitch dynamics.
Step 2

URDF to ROS 2 & Gazebo

The URDF is parameterized into modular XACRO files and extended with Gazebo simulation plugins:

  • DiffDrive Plugin: Configured gz-sim-diff-drive-system to command wheel joints from /cmd_vel and calculate simulated wheel odometry.
  • Parameterization: Modular macros allow adjusting wheel track width ($0.582\text{ m}$), wheel radius ($0.165\text{ m}$), and sensor mount offsets.
  • ROS 2 Launch Integration: Automated launch via robot_state_publisher publishing joint states and spawning the entity using ros_gz_sim.
Step 3

TF Transform Tree Implementation

The tf2 coordinate hierarchy establishes real-time spatial transformations between all links:

  • Tree Hierarchy: map → odom → base_footprint → base_link → sensor frames (laser_link, imu_link, gps_link, camera_link) and wheel joints.
  • Spatial Synchronization: When the LiDAR detects an obstacle 2 meters ahead in laser_link, the TF tree instantly projects those range points into base_link and the global map, allowing Nav2 costmaps to inflate obstacles accurately.
Stage 2 • Physics Engine

2.0 Simulating Real-World Constraints & Physics Dynamics

DART/ODE • $\mu = 0.8$

Simulation fidelity depends entirely on modeling contact forces, ground surface friction, and vehicle rolling resistance. The simulation uses Gazebo Harmonic (gz-sim 8.x) with the DART/ODE physics backend configured at a $1000\text{ Hz}$ update frequency ($\Delta t_{\text{step}} = 1.0\text{ ms}$).

Rolling Resistance ($c_{rr} \approx 0.015 - 0.02$)

Pneumatic tires undergo continuous viscoelastic deformation at the tarmac contact patch, creating a resistive rolling force $F_{rr} = c_{rr} M g$. Modeling this parameter ensures the vehicle decelerates realistically upon releasing throttle rather than coasting frictionlessly.

Tarmac Coulomb Friction ($\mu = 0.8$)

Ground contact parameters were tuned to Coulomb static friction $\mu_s = 0.85$, dynamic friction $\mu_k = 0.75$, and restitution coefficient $e = 0.05$, matching empirical measurements of dry aerodrome asphalt and smooth concrete campus walkways.

Skid-Steer Scrubbing Moment ($M_{z,\text{scrub}}$)

When a 4WD rover turns in place, all four wheels are forced to drag sideways against the tarmac, generating a severe resistive yaw moment that must be overcome by the motors.

Mathematical Proof: Total Vehicle Yaw Scrubbing Moment ($M_{z,\text{scrub}}$)

For vehicle mass $M = 65.0\text{ kg}$, tarmac friction $\mu = 0.80$, gravity $g = 9.81\text{ m/s}^2$, and wheelbase $L = 0.50\text{ m}$:

$$M_{z,\text{scrub}} = \frac{1}{2}\mu M g L = \frac{1}{2}(0.80)(65.0\text{ kg})(9.81\text{ m/s}^2)(0.50\text{ m}) = \mathbf{127.5\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}}$$

Overcoming this resistive moment requires a static breakaway torque of $\tau_{\text{skid}} \approx 19.73\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ per motor shaft. For hub motor torque constant $K_t \approx 1.057\text{ N}\cdot\text{m/A}$, executing an in-place turn from a dead stop would draw $74.64\text{ A}$ across the 48V bus, exceeding the $70.0\text{ A}$ BMS safety cutoff. This physics validation demonstrates that zero-radius pivot turns must be prevented through continuous smooth-curvature trajectory planning, eliminating tire scrubbing and protecting motor windings from thermal overload.

Stage 3 • Sensors

3.0 Sensors: Purpose, Perception & Synthetic Simulation

LiDAR • IMU • RTK-GPS

The digital twin generates synthetic sensor streams matching physical sensor rates, message schemas, and noise profiles, routed seamlessly into ROS 2 Jazzy through ros_gz_bridge:

📡 2D / 3D LiDAR (/scan)

Purpose: Dynamic obstacle detection (foreign object debris, birds, service carts, pedestrians) and local costmap inflation for proactive collision avoidance.

Simulation: Modeled as a Hokuyo UST-20LX equivalent with $360^\circ$ planar coverage, $1080$ ray beams ($0.33^\circ$ angular resolution), $25.0\text{ m}$ range, and zero-mean Gaussian noise ($\sigma = 0.01\text{ m}$) at $20\text{ Hz}$.

🧭 Inertial Measurement Unit (/imu/data)

Purpose: Measures 9-DOF orientation, high-frequency ($100-200\text{ Hz}$) angular yaw rates, and linear accelerations, bridging latency gaps between lower-frequency GPS updates.

Simulation: Gazebo IMU plugin with calibrated angular random walk, gyro bias drift, and accelerometer white noise modeling chassis vibration over pavement joints.

📍 RTK-GNSS Receiver (/gps/fix)

Purpose: Provides centimeter-level absolute global coordinates ($x, y, z$, lat/lon) in WGS84, delivering drift-free localization across expansive runways where LiDAR SLAM fails.

Simulation: Gazebo NavSat plugin configured with georeferenced origin matching NUS EA Field coordinates ($1.3000^\circ\text{N}, 103.7712^\circ\text{E}$).

📷 Forward RGB Camera (/camera/image_raw)

Purpose: Operator visual stream and optical confirmation of runway debris feeding edge AI (YOLO) detection models.

Simulation: GPU camera shader delivering $1280 \times 720$ resolution at $30\text{ FPS}$ with a $90^\circ$ horizontal field of view (HFOV).

Sensor Fusion Pipeline: Dual-EKF Architecture (robot_localization)

Localization fuses disparate sensor rates via a two-stage Extended Kalman Filter:
• Local EKF (odom frame): Fuses high-rate wheel odometry ($50\text{ Hz}$) with IMU angular velocity ($100\text{ Hz}$) to produce a smooth, continuous, drift-minimizing pose estimate for local trajectory controllers.
• Global EKF (map frame): Fuses local odometry with absolute RTK-GNSS position fixes ($10\text{ Hz}$), eliminating cumulative wheel-slip drift and anchoring the robot within world coordinates.

The digital twin validates three primary navigation algorithms running under the ROS 2 Jazzy Nav2 stack:

Global Planner (SmacPlanner)

Enforces continuous-curvature trajectory primitives, ensuring planned paths avoid high-current zero-radius pivot scrub on high-friction tarmac.

Local Controller (Regulated Pure Pursuit)

Dynamically scales linear velocity based on path curvature ($\kappa$) and proximity to obstacles, preventing high-speed cornering roll and wheel slip.

Collision Monitor (Safety Interlock)

Enforces dual-zone protection polygons ($3.5\text{ m}$ proactive slowdown, $0.8\text{ m}$ emergency stop), arresting motion within $2.715\text{ m}$ from $10\text{ km/h}$.

5.1 Real-World Challenges Anticipated in Semester 2 Physical Deployment

While the Phase 1 digital twin demonstrated $RTF = 0.98 \pm 0.02$ and verified trajectory stability in software, transitioning to physical hardware in Semester 2 introduces critical physical discrepancies (the "Sim-to-Real Gap"):

1. Actuator Dynamics & CAN Bus Latency

In Gazebo, wheel velocities update instantaneously. The physical ZLAC8015D BLDC hub motors communicate over a CANopen bus via STM32 firmware with 10–20 ms loop latency, non-linear torque-speed drop-off curves, and current limitations that require feedforward tuning.

2. Unmodeled Wheel Slip & Variable Friction

Gazebo's uniform Coulomb friction ($\mu = 0.8$) does not capture real-world pavement variations such as wet tarmac patches, aircraft rubber touchdown streaks, loose gravel, or tire tread wear, inducing unpredictable lateral drift during high-speed braking sweeps.

3. Suspension Dynamics & Sensor Noise

The physical custom aluminum trailing-arm suspension transmits terrain vibrations into the IMU accelerometer and induces high-frequency pitch jitter on the 2D LiDAR plane, requiring Butterworth low-pass filtering.

4. RTK Multipath & Satellite Occlusion

Near airport hangars and campus building facades, satellite signal multipath can cause RTK fix dropouts. The Dual-EKF must implement covariance threshold gating to fall back smoothly onto wheel odometry and IMU integration.

5. Over-the-Air RF Telemetry Latency

Replacing virtual DDS local loopbacks with the physical Microhard P900 V2 900 MHz FHSS serial radio introduces packet jitter and bandwidth constraints (115200 bps) over 1+ km sweeps, requiring lightweight binary JSON serialization.

Engineering Retrospective

🛠️ Summary: Major Challenges Faced & Engineering Solutions

4 Primary Hurdle Resolves

Simulating an autonomous Hunter in wide-area aerodrome and outdoor environments presented four core engineering challenges that do not occur in conventional indoor mobile robotics:

1. Open Runway Environment Environment
  • Flat and expansive open space ($> 60\text{ m} \times 100\text{ m}$).
  • Zero vertical reference walls or columns within LiDAR range ($25\text{ m}$).
  • Longitudinal scan covariance diverges ($\Sigma_{xx} \to \infty$), causing 2D SLAM scan matching to slip and fail.
Solution: Orthophoto Aerial Mapping
  • Import calibrated high-resolution aerial orthophoto ($0.05\text{ m/pixel}$).
  • Annotate static traversable lanes and geofenced non-traversable boundaries.
  • Eliminates reliance on vertical laser scan structures for map generation.
2. Finding a Model Kinematics
  • Custom chassis fabrication was ongoing in parallel with autonomy development.
  • Simulation required a verified 4WD skid-steer model with full TF tree frames.
  • Mass distribution and hub motor kinematics had to match the physical design.
Solution: AgileX Scout 2.0 Baseline Proxy
  • Selected AgileX Scout 2.0 as verified commercial mobile robot proxy.
  • Matches $65.0\text{ kg}$ payload, $L/B = 0.855$ ratio, and 48V hub motor drive.
  • Allowed full autonomy and C2 software stack to mature in Semester 1.
3. Mapping Scale SLAM Limits
  • Airfield runways span 3–4 km in length and 60 m in width.
  • Dynamic occupancy grid SLAM mapping across this scale exhausts edge computer RAM.
  • High-resolution grid updates cause CPU throttling and frame rate collapse.
Solution: Pre-Computed Static Costmaps
  • Generate pre-annotated static costmaps (free lanes = cost 0, grass verges = cost 254).
  • Downsample grid cells to $5.0\text{ cm/pixel}$ balancing planning precision with low RAM.
  • Allows Nav2 global planners to compute full-mission paths instantaneously.
4. Localisation Drifting State Estimation
  • 4WD skid-steer wheel odometry inherently suffers cumulative lateral wheel slip.
  • Turning on high-friction tarmac produces severe dead-reckoning drift.
  • LiDAR scan matching is unavailable due to open aerodrome terrain geometry.
Solution: Dual-EKF Sensor Fusion
  • Dual-EKF architecture fusing wheel odometry, 9-DOF IMU, and RTK-GNSS positioning.
  • Local EKF maintains smooth $50\text{ Hz}$ control odometry in odom frame.
  • Global EKF anchors pose to absolute WGS84 coordinates in map frame, eliminating drift.

6.0 Multi-Machine Command-and-Control (C2) Integration

Hardware Ingest • Static CycloneDDS Unicast

The Software-in-the-Loop testbench bridges two independent physical computing platforms to replicate the real-world UAV-to-Hunter coordination datalink:

Transmitter Node (RPi 5 / UAV C2 Ground Station)

Static IP: 192.168.137.233. Executes Charan's UAV C2 alert program (DemoComs_C2.py) and Hilbert's standalone C2 Tactical GUI (uav_waypoint_commander.py). Formats and publishes asynchronous JSON target alerts on /c2/target_alert.

Receiver Node (Laptop / Gazebo Hunter Platform)

Static IP: 192.168.137.1. Runs Gazebo Harmonic physics simulation, Nav2 SmacPlanner, and Hilbert's ugv_c2_bridge_node.py, ingesting alerts and driving Hunter to intercept coordinates.

CycloneDDS Static Unicast Discovery Configuration (cyclonedds.xml)

Standard DDS multicast discovery is blocked by router IGMP snooping. To establish deterministic peer discovery, explicit unicast XML peering was configured: 

<?xml version="1.0" encoding="UTF-8" ?>
<CycloneDDS xmlns="https://cdds.io/config">
  <Domain id="any">
    <General>
      <NetworkInterface name="auto"/>
      <AllowMulticast>false</AllowMulticast>
    </General>
    <Discovery>
      <Peers>
        <Peer address="192.168.137.1"/>
        <Peer address="192.168.137.233"/>
      </Peers>
      <ParticipantIndex>auto</ParticipantIndex>
    </Discovery>
  </Domain>
</CycloneDDS>
