Subsystem Summary: The FOD Hunter multi-agent communication framework coordinates an aerial UAV scout with a ground UGV rover across a $5\text{ km} \times 60\text{ m}$ active airfield strip. To comply with military airbase security and overcome tarmac RF multipath fading, the architecture eliminates uncompressed video streaming and implements an air-gapped, asynchronous serial telemetry link powered by the Microhard P900 V2 (917–925 MHz FHSS). This specification summarizes the operational workflow, comparative hardware evaluation, decision justifications, ROS 2 middleware bridging, and the phased implementation roadmap.
1. Multi-Agent Operational Flow & System Architecture

The heterogeneous multi-agent architecture executes four synchronized operational stages to rapidly detect and clear foreign object debris (FOD) with minimal runway disruption:

Stage 1 UAV Aerial Scout (Macro)
  • High-speed lawnmower sweep across the $5\text{ km}$ runway at $30\text{--}40\text{ km/h}$.
  • Onboard edge AI (YOLOv8/TensorRT) detects and classifies candidate FOD.
  • Multi-frequency RTK-GNSS logs geodetic WGS84 coordinates ($\pm 0.02\text{ m}$).
Stage 2 Secure C2 Handshake
  • Air-gapped non-internet RF link via Microhard P900 V2.
  • Serializes encrypted JSON metadata packets ($< 2\text{ KB}$) with target coordinates.
  • Bi-directional heartbeat and telemetry monitoring prevent link loss.
Stage 3 UGV Precision Transit
  • navsat_transform_node converts WGS84 to local metric map coordinates.
  • ROS 2 Nav2 stack (SmacPlannerHybrid) computes kinematically compliant paths.
  • UGV sprints directly to cued coordinates at speeds up to $10\text{ km/h}$.
Stage 4 Verification & Remediation
  • Co-axial 3D LiDAR (Livox Mid-360) and camera confirm object height profile.
  • Active remediation mechanism engages (vacuum hood & pickup module).
  • Mission ledger update (COLLECTED) transmitted to Base Station C2.
2. Operational & Environmental Constraints

Deploying autonomous robotics across active military airbases and civil airport flight lines imposes four non-negotiable operational requirements:

Constraint Parameter Operational Specification Engineering Justification
Wireless Mobility Untethered peer-to-peer telemetry link Permits continuous, independent physical motion between aerial and surface robotic agents.
Long Operational Range Line-of-Sight coverage of 1 to 4 km Must span the full length of military runways, blast pads, and surrounding taxiways.
Air-Gapped / Non-Internet Zero reliance on commercial 4G/5G, clouds, or ISPs Enforces military OPSEC; eliminates electronic warfare risks, base tower dead zones, and IP hijacking.
Tarmac Hardening Immunity to multipath fading & tropical rain Must maintain SNR despite severe asphalt RF reflections and thermal tarmac shimmer.
3. Inter-Agent Telemetry Protocol: Why Asynchronous Packets?
Bandwidth Design Principle: Continuously streaming high-definition video over multi-kilometer RF saturates wireless link budgets, induces multi-second queue latency, and drains the aerial platform's battery. Instead, computer vision inference runs locally at the edge on the UAV's NVIDIA Jetson, transmitting only asynchronous, structured JSON C2 alerts (< 2 KB).

Operational & Empirically Verified

For Phase 1 flight line deployment, the team engineered and implemented a standalone, ultra-lightweight C2 Tactical HUD (uav_waypoint_commander.py) running directly on the Raspberry Pi 5 tactical field ground station. Key design justifications include:

⚡ Deterministic Compute Footprint

Draws under 4% CPU and ~45 MB RAM while maintaining a steady 30 FPS render loop on the RPi 5 touchscreen, preventing thermal throttling in high ambient airfield conditions.

🎯 Airside Mission Abstractions

Encapsulates complex ROS 2 actions into single-click physical buttons: instant FOD threat interception, sequential multi-point sweep lines, physical emergency stop, and automated reverse home docking.

🔒 Direct DDS Unicast Reliability

Subscribes directly to native CycloneDDS unicast endpoints without intermediary WebSocket bridge servers, Node.js daemons, or cloud dependencies that could fail under tarmac RF interference.

Architectural Verdict Is RViz 2 a Choice for UAV-UGV Collaboration? Do We Need It?
Disqualified for Collaborative Field Operations

Engineering Verdict: No, RViz 2 is fundamentally unsuitable for collaborative multi-agent C2. While RViz 2 is the gold standard for developer-level algorithm debugging, it is not an operational command-and-control tool. The team has strictly disqualified it from field operations based on three fundamental architectural limitations:

1. Single-Robot Centric Design

RViz 2's coordinate tree is architected around a single fixed frame (e.g. odom or map). Visualizing heterogeneous multi-agent systems (UAV + UGV) causes TF frame clutter, requires complex namespace remapping (/uav/tf, /ugv/tf), and lacks native multi-robot tracking views.

2. Zero High-Level C2 & Safety Abstractions

RViz 2 exposes hundreds of low-level developer dropdowns (costmap voxel decay, TF axes, laser point decay). It lacks basic airfield operational primitives: no waypoint dispatch queue, no FOD triage alerts, no battery state of charge, no vacuum suction status, and no reverse docking trigger.

3. Prohibitive Resource Overhead on SBCs

Its monolithic C++/Qt6/OGRE 3D engine consumes 50–80% CPU and >450 MB RAM on a Raspberry Pi 5. Over remote X11/VNC links, rendering stutters severely, introducing unacceptable visual latency during active runway sweeps.

Role in the Project: We strictly restrict RViz 2 to benchtop developer diagnostics on an autonomy engineer's laptop (e.g., verifying Livox LiDAR TF transforms and tuning Nav2 costmap inflation radii). It has no role in collaborative UAV-UGV field operations.
Phase 2 Strategic Choice Trade Study: Foxglove Studio vs. Rerun.io
Permanent Airbase Fleet Operations

For Phase 2 (scaling to permanent multi-seat airfield control shelters, multi-drone coverage, and long-range runway sweeps), the operational requirement shifts from an ultra-compact field SBC HUD to an enterprise visualization and analytical framework. The trade study compares the two leading next-generation robotics frameworks:

Foxglove Studio: The Operational Fleet C2 Choice Recommended for Live C2
  • Zero-Install Multi-Seat Browser Client: Powered by foxglove_bridge (WebSocket), airfield control tower staff can monitor live multi-agent sweeps on standard laptops, tablets, or wall displays without installing ROS 2 or Linux.
  • Native 2D Navigation & Costmaps: Out-of-the-box rendering of nav_msgs/OccupancyGrid, local/global costmaps, and RTK-GNSS coordinates overlaid directly on high-resolution satellite runway tiles (Mapbox / Bing).
  • Bidirectional Action Dispatch: Supports custom UI panels to dispatch Nav2 navigation goals, trigger service calls (vacuum toggle), and execute remote emergency stop directly from dashboard buttons.
  • Integrated Multi-Modal Dashboards: Flawlessly synchronizes camera feeds, Livox LiDAR clouds, and time-series telemetry plots (battery discharge, motor current, RF RSSI) in custom drag-and-drop layouts.
Phase 2 Suitability: Ideal for real-time airbase mission control and remote fleet monitoring where multi-user browser accessibility and interactive robot dispatch are paramount.
Rerun.io: The Algorithmic Forensic Choice Unmatched for Post-Mortem
  • Sub-Millisecond Temporal Scrubbing: Powered by Rust, WGPU, and Apache Arrow ECS, Rerun enables autonomy engineers to scrub backward frame-by-frame across synchronized sensor streams to audit complex airfield anomalies.
  • Unrivaled 3D Point Cloud Throughput: Renders millions of raw LiDAR points, camera frames, and bounding boxes at steady 60 FPS with virtually zero rendering stutter or memory leaks.
  • Language-Agnostic Logging SDK: Extremely clean Python/C++/Rust APIs that decouple multimodal logging from ROS 2 middleware lock-in.
  • Key Operational Constraint: Completely read-only. It lacks native interactive action dispatch (cannot send Nav2 goals or teleoperate robots) and lacks out-of-the-box satellite tile map overlays.
Phase 2 Suitability: Unmatched for offline algorithmic forensics, edge-case YOLO false negative audits, and tarmac wheel slippage analysis, but cannot serve as a standalone operational C2.
Phase 2 Architectural Decision Synthesis: For live airfield operations and tower control, Foxglove Studio is the superior choice due to its web-native multi-seat accessibility, native Nav2 costmap support, and bidirectional action dispatch. Conversely, Rerun.io is the designated tool for offline post-mission algorithmic forensics. In the final Phase 2 architecture, the two tools serve complementary roles: Foxglove Studio handles real-time mission execution in the tower, while recorded MCAP/Arrow datasets are ingested into Rerun.io for sub-millisecond timeline post-mortems and AI model retraining.

7. Hardware Selection Justification: Why Microhard P900 V2?

The project selected the Microhard P900 V2 serial RF datalink over broadband IP alternatives (e.g. pMDDL2550) based on four critical technical justifications:

SELECTED HARDWARE DATALINK

Microhard P900 V2

Long-Range 900 MHz FHSS Serial Data Module

  • Band: 917–925 MHz (IMDA TS SRD Singapore Compliant)
  • Power / Sensitivity: Up to 1W (+30 dBm) / -116 dBm
  • Interface: Direct Point-to-Point UART Serial (`/dev/ttyUSB0`)
  • Weight & Power: Only 18 g and < 2.5 W draw
  • Range: Exceeds 5 km Line-of-Sight across open pavement
EVALUATED BROADBAND ALTERNATIVE

Microhard pMDDL2550

Dual-Band 2.4 / 5.8 GHz OFDM Broadband IP Bridge

  • Bandwidth: Up to 25 Mbps (supports streaming uncompressed video)
  • System Overhead: Requires industrial Ethernet switch & dual MIMO antennas
  • RF Attenuation: High 2.4/5.8 GHz atmospheric & wet asphalt absorption
  • Weight & Power: Heavy 130 g module drawing > 10 W
  • Cost: Approx. 4× higher capital cost

1. Bandwidth Sizing & Transmission Latency

Because vision processing is localized at the edge, transmission is restricted to compact $1.5\text{ KB}$ JSON alerts. At standard $115.2\text{ kbps}$ baud rate:
Latency = (1.5 KB × 8 bits) / 115.2 kbps ≈ 104 ms. Handoff completes in ~100 ms, providing real-time response without needing megabits of bandwidth.

2. Superior Sub-GHz RF Propagation

The 900 MHz band offers much longer wavelengths (~33 cm) compared to 2.4 GHz (~12.5 cm) and 5.8 GHz (~5.2 cm). This minimizes signal attenuation through tropical downpours and significantly reduces destructive multipath reflection off flat asphalt surfaces.

3. SWaP-C Optimization (Size, Weight, Power, Cost)

The P900 V2 weighs just 18 g and consumes < 2.5 W, preserving UAV flight endurance. It eliminates the need for bulky $2\times 2\text{ MIMO}$ dual antenna separation, DC–DC buck converters, and onboard Ethernet network switches, saving substantial weight and cost.

4. Direct ROS 2 Node Serial Interfacing

Operating as a character serial device (UART / `/dev/ttyUSB0`), the P900 directly streams byte buffers through a lightweight ROS 2 Python serial node, avoiding PPP daemon encapsulation overhead, IP fragmentation, and multicast DDS discovery storms across the radio link.

8. Singapore RF Regulatory Compliance (IMDA TS SRD)
Statutory Spectrum Enforcement: Radio operations in Singapore must strictly adhere to statutory requirements governed by the Infocomm Media Development Authority (IMDA) under the Telecommunications Act 1999:
  • Authorized Frequency Band: While default P900 transceivers hop across the US FCC band (902–928 MHz), Singapore regulations strictly limit unlicensed Short Range Devices to 917–925 MHz.
  • Cellular Interference Prevention: Frequencies between 902–917 MHz overlap directly with licensed commercial cellular mobile operator uplinks. Transmitting in this band causes severe illegal out-of-band interference.
  • Firmware Lock & Power Throttling: P900 frequency-hopping tables are software-locked via AT commands strictly to the 917–925 MHz sub-band. RF output power is throttled to ensure radiated power stays strictly within the statutory limit of $\le 100\text{ mW}$ ($+20\text{ dBm}$) EIRP.
9. ROS 2 Middleware & Software Bridge Architecture

The system implements an architectural decoupling separating long-range inter-agent serial communications from intra-vehicle high-speed DDS middleware:

🔄 Decoupled Two-Layer Communication Model

1. Inter-Agent Air Link (UAV ↔ UGV)

Operates via custom ROS 2 serial bridge nodes (uav_serial_bridge_node and ugv_c2_receiver_node) communicating over /dev/ttyUSB0 using pyserial. Transmits encrypted JSON payloads with CRC-16 checksums over 917–925 MHz FHSS.

2. Intra-Vehicle Local Middleware

Onboard each robot, standard ROS 2 Data Distribution Service (CycloneDDS) operates over local loopback (127.0.0.1) and high-speed onboard Ethernet, seamlessly interconnecting Jetson Orin Nano, Livox Mid-360 LiDAR, microcontrollers, and Nav2 nodes.

🗺️ Coordinate Transformation & Nav2 Integration Pipeline

Global geodetic coordinates received from the UAV are automatically transformed into metric frames for autonomous ground navigation:

WGS84 (Lat, Lon, Alt) ➔ navsat_transform_node ➔ Metric Goal Pose (X, Y) map ➔ Nav2 SmacPlanner ➔ odom ➔ base_link
  • navsat_transform_node converts WGS84 coordinates into metric geometry_msgs/PoseStamped in the global ENU frame (map) and dispatches target goals to Nav2.
  • ekf_node_global fuses wheel odometry, 9-DOF IMU data, and RTK-GNSS fixes to publish the dynamic map ➔ odom transform.
  • ekf_node_local computes smooth, continuous odom ➔ base_link tracking at 50 Hz for local costmaps and velocity control (cmd_vel).

11. Fail-Safe Protocols & Cybersecurity Program
Timeout > 5.0 s Safety Standby

If keep-alive heartbeat pulses lapse beyond 5.0 s, the UGV executes controlled deceleration to 0 RPM and holds position.

Shadowing Buffer Task Retention

Previously dispatched target waypoints remain buffered in local memory, enabling completion of active clearance during brief RF shadowing.

Timeout > 30.0 s Autonomous RTB

If link interruption persists beyond 30 s, the UGV cancels active mission queues and navigates autonomously to the designated airside staging lane.

Crypto Security AES-256 & HMAC

RF transmissions utilize AES-256 encryption, FHSS modulation, and SHA-256 signatures to reject rogue commands or electronic spoofing.
