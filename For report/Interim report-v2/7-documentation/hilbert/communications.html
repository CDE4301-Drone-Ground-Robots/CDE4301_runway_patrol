1.0 Subsystem Scope (A1)

1.0 Subsystem Scope & Operational Airfield Mandates

Airfield Bottleneck • Multi-Agent Mandates

1.1 Airfield Detect-to-Clear Bottleneck & Multi-Agent Rationale

Foreign Object Debris (FOD) on active military and commercial runways presents a critical operational vulnerability (FAA AC 150/5220-24 [2]; Shan et al., 2025 [12]). Conventional airside maintenance relies on manual human drive-by inspections, requiring 30 to 45 minutes of total runway shutdown. This operational bottleneck induces severe commercial flight diversion penalties and impairs rapid military scramble readiness.

Single-agent autonomous systems fail to resolve this operational trade-off: a ground vehicle traveling at safe sweep speeds ($10\text{--}15\text{ km/h}$) cannot inspect a $5.0\text{ km} \times 60\text{ m}$ runway within narrow air traffic windows, whereas an aerial drone possesses high search velocities ($30\text{--}40\text{ km/h}$) but lacks physical payload mass to clear heavy metallic debris. This necessitates a heterogeneous multi-agent architecture (Kim et al., 2019 [5]; Multimodal Transportation, 2025 [13]):

Aerial Reconnaissance Agent (UAV Scout)

Executes high-speed aerial lawnmower passes at $35\text{ km/h}$ over the runway surface, scanning a $5\text{ km}$ corridor in under 9 minutes. Onboard edge AI (YOLOv8) detects candidate debris, tags geodetic coordinates ($\pm 0.02\text{ m}$ WGS84), and broadcasts asynchronous C2 target alerts.

Surface Remediation Agent (UGV Rover)

Stands by in an airside staging lane. Upon ingesting a C2 target alert, Nav2 computes a sprint trajectory directly to the cued coordinates, conducts optical verification, engages high-flow suction/magnetic remediation, and updates the shared mission ledger.

1.3 Simulation Chassis Proxy Rationale: AgileX Scout 2.0 Selection vs. Custom CAD

In Phase 1, development focused strictly on the Software-in-the-Loop (SIL) simulation digital twin. To validate the inter-agent C2 data link, goal queue handling, and Nav2 action server pipelines without being blocked by mechanical fabrication, the team selected the AgileX Scout 2.0 proxy model in Gazebo Harmonic over an unverified raw CAD export based on three fundamental engineering justifications:

1. Decoupling Software Autonomy from Hardware Fabrication Lead Times

Custom chassis mechanical CAD design, structural FEA load analysis, waterjet aluminum cutting, and CNC machining of the vacuum suction hood are progressing concurrently in Phase 1. If software autonomy, multi-agent C2 communications, and Nav2 trajectory planning were forced to wait for physical chassis assembly and electrical harness routing, software validation would have stalled for 3 to 4 months. Using an industry-verified simulation proxy eliminated this critical-path dependency.

2. Identical 4WD Skid-Steer Kinematics & Turning Scrub Dynamics

Both the custom UGV and the AgileX Scout 2.0 share identical four-wheel independent drive skid-steer kinematics, track width ratios ($B \approx 0.58\text{ m}$), wheel base ($L \approx 0.50\text{ m}$), and turning scrub dynamics. The differential velocity mapping matrix $\begin{bmatrix} v_L \\ v_R \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -B/2 \\ 1 & B/2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} v \\ \omega \end{bmatrix}$, instantaneous centers of rotation (ICR), and skid-steer friction torque are mathematically equivalent. Consequently, C2 goal ingestion and Nav2 controller plugins transfer to the physical rover with zero architectural rework.

3. Certified Dynamic Inertia Tensors & ODE Surface Friction

The AgileX Scout 2.0 URDF incorporates certified mass properties ($M = 65.0\text{ kg}$), principal moments of inertia ($I_{xx}, I_{yy}, I_{zz}$), and ODE contact surface friction coefficients ($\mu_s = 0.85, \mu_k = 0.75$), preventing unrealistic wheel slip artifacts during high-speed C2 waypoint dispatch and emergency deceleration maneuvers (Siegwart et al., 2011 [14]; Gillespie, 1992 [15]).

2.0 Architectural Iterations & Engineering Progression

4 Phased Milestones • Hardwired to Long-Range RF

The C2 communications framework evolved through four deliberate engineering iterations, systematically eliminating middleware race conditions, reducing RF airtime, and resolving the boundary between inter-agent serial links and intra-vehicle DDS:

Iteration 1 • Tested & Verified Benchtop Baseline

Point-to-Point Hardwired Link & Middleware Baseline (Direct RJ45)

Direct Category 6 RJ45 connection between Raspberry Pi 5 (`192.168.137.233`) and Laptop (`192.168.137.1`). CycloneDDS with explicit unicast peer discovery (`cyclonedds.xml`) was used exclusively for Phase 1 Interim Benchtop testing, eliminating router hops and establishing baseline serialization performance ($1.84\text{ ms}$ latency, $0.0\%$ packet loss) (Macenski et al., 2022 [7]).

Iteration 2 • Tested & Verified Protocol Decoupling

Local Wireless Subnet & Protocol Decoupling (Over Local Wi-Fi Router)

Transitioned both platforms onto an air-gapped 5 GHz Wi-Fi router. Empirical testing confirmed that transmitting DDS discovery packets over fluctuating wireless links triggers discovery storms and UDP drop under multipath fading. This proved that inter-agent communications must not rely on DDS, driving the architectural decision to decouple inter-agent traffic into asynchronous, serial JSON alert payloads ($< 1.5\text{ KB}$).

Iteration 3 • Simulated & Modeled Spatial Math

Geodetic-to-Cartesian Spatial Transformation Pipeline

Engineered the mathematical coordinate conversion pipeline. Formulated the Transverse Mercator projection from WGS84 coordinates into UTM Zone 48N, followed by a 2D affine rotation into the local metric airfield map frame, validated across Gazebo digital twin waypoints (Dolgov et al., 2010 [19]).

Iteration 4 • Hardware Roadmap Phase 2 RF

Long-Range RF Telemetry & Statutory Transition (Microhard P900 V2)

Hardware procurement roadmap replacing Wi-Fi with the Microhard P900 V2. Across the air, data travels strictly as serial JSON packets over UART (`/dev/ttyUSB0`) with a dedicated Python bridge node (`serial_c2_bridge`). CycloneDDS remains active solely as intra-vehicle middleware inside the UGV chassis over local loopback (`127.0.0.1`), ensuring zero radio discovery overhead while maintaining statutory compliance with IMDA TS SRD ($917\text{--}925\text{ MHz}$, $\le 100\text{ mW}$ EIRP) [3].

3.2.1 Serialization Latency & Airtime Link Budget Derivation

At a standard serial baud rate $R_{\text{baud}} = 115{,}200\text{ bps}$ (8 data bits, 1 stop bit, no parity, effective transfer rate $11{,}520\text{ bytes/s}$), the serialization latency $T_{\text{ser}}$ for a maximum packet size $L_{\text{packet}} = 1{,}500\text{ bytes}$ is derived as:

$$T_{\text{ser}} = \frac{L_{\text{packet}} \times 10\text{ bits}}{R_{\text{baud}}} = \frac{1500 \times 10}{115200} = 0.1302\text{ s} \approx 130.2\text{ ms}$$

At $230{,}400\text{ bps}$, serialization drops to $\mathbf{65.1\text{ ms}}$, delivering sub-100 ms alert dispatch across the full $4.0\text{ km}$ runway length.

Next, we derive the Free-Space Path Loss ($FSPL$) and link margin across the maximum required operating distance $d = 4000\text{ m}$ ($4.0\text{ km}$):

$$FSPL(\text{dB}) = 20\log_{10}(d) + 20\log_{10}(f) + 20\log_{10}\left(\frac{4\pi}{c}\right) = 20\log_{10}(4000) + 20\log_{10}(920 \times 10^6) - 147.55 = 103.77\text{ dB}$$

By comparison, for 2.4 GHz and 5.8 GHz broadband IP bridges:

  • $FSPL_{2.4\text{ GHz}} = 103.77 + 20\log_{10}(2400 / 920) = \mathbf{112.10\text{ dB}}$ ($+8.33\text{ dB}$ additional path loss, requiring $\approx 6.8\times$ transmit power).
  • $FSPL_{5.8\text{ GHz}} = 103.77 + 20\log_{10}(5800 / 920) = \mathbf{119.77\text{ dB}}$ ($+16.00\text{ dB}$ additional path loss, requiring $\approx 40\times$ transmit power).

The link fade margin $M_{\text{fade}}$ under the IMDA statutory cap ($P_{\text{TX}} = +20\text{ dBm}$ EIRP), with $2.15\text{ dBi}$ dipole antennas ($G_{\text{TX}} = G_{\text{RX}} = 2.15\text{ dBi}$), cable losses $L_{\text{cable}} = 1.0\text{ dB}$, and receiver sensitivity $P_{\text{RX\_sens}} = -116\text{ dBm}$ (Microhard P900 V2 specification) is calculated as:

$$M_{\text{fade}} = P_{\text{TX}} + G_{\text{TX}} + G_{\text{RX}} - FSPL - L_{\text{cable}} - P_{\text{RX\_sens}} = 20 + 2.15 + 2.15 - 103.77 - 1.0 - (-116) = \mathbf{+35.53\text{ dB}}$$

A link fade margin of $\mathbf{+35.5\text{ dB}}$ provides an extraordinary buffer exceeding the standard aeronautical engineering requirement ($\ge 15.0\text{ dB}$), guaranteeing zero packet loss even during heavy tropical thunderstorms.

3.2.2 Power Sizing & Packaging Overhead (< 2.5 W vs. Industrial Switch Rails)

The Microhard P900 V2 draws only $2.3\text{ W}$ ($460\text{ mA}$ at $5.0\text{ V}$) at peak transmit power ($+20\text{ dBm}$) and weighs just $18\text{ g}$. It interfaces directly with the onboard computer via UART serial (`/dev/ttyUSB0` or TTL). Conversely, broadband IP bridges (pMDDL2550) require > $10.5\text{ W}$, weigh $130\text{ g}$, demand dedicated 12V buck regulators, and necessitate an onboard industrial Ethernet switch ($+350\text{ g}$, $+15\text{ W}$), severely compromising the UAV’s flight endurance.

3.2.3 Sub-1 GHz RF Propagation & Asphalt Diffraction Performance

The first Fresnel zone radius $r_1$ midway along the $4.0\text{ km}$ link ($d_1 = d_2 = 2000\text{ m}$) at frequency $f = 920\text{ MHz}$ ($\lambda = c/f = 0.326\text{ m}$) is given by:

$$r_1 = \sqrt{\frac{\lambda \cdot d_1 \cdot d_2}{d_1 + d_2}} = \sqrt{\frac{0.326 \times 2000 \times 2000}{4000}} = \mathbf{18.06\text{ m}}$$

With the UAV operating at survey altitude $H_{\text{UAV}} = 25\text{ m}$ and the UGV antenna mounted at $H_{\text{UGV}} = 0.85\text{ m}$ above the tarmac, the line of sight maintains a continuous positive elevation angle ($> 0.35^\circ$). At $920\text{ MHz}$, the long wavelength ($\lambda = 32.6\text{ cm}$) diffracts smoothly over runway camber and asphalt seams, whereas $2.4\text{ GHz}$ ($\lambda = 12.5\text{ cm}$) and $5.8\text{ GHz}$ ($\lambda = 5.2\text{ cm}$) experience destructive ground-reflection multipath cancellations that create blind spots along the flight line.

3.3 Singapore Spectrum Compliance: IMDA TS SRD Window (917–925 MHz, ≤ 100 mW EIRP)

Radio frequency transmissions across military and civil airfields in Singapore are strictly governed by the Infocomm Media Development Authority (IMDA) under the Telecommunications Act 1999 [11] and the Technical Specification for Short Range Devices (IMDA TS SRD Issue 1 Rev 2, 2021 [3]), Table 1 (Item 13b):

Critical Statutory Constraint: FCC vs. IMDA Frequency Windows

Off-the-shelf North American Microhard P900 modules factory-default to the US FCC band ($902\text{--}928\text{ MHz}$). In Singapore, frequencies between $902\text{--}917\text{ MHz}$ overlap directly with licensed commercial cellular mobile operator uplinks (Singtel, StarHub, M1). Transmitting in this band constitutes an illegal out-of-band broadcast subject to immediate statutory confiscation and criminal fines under Singapore law.

  • Permitted Operational Window: Transmissions are strictly locked to the $917\text{--}925\text{ MHz}$ SRD sub-band.
  • Maximum Effective Radiated Power (EIRP): Radio firmware is throttled to guarantee $\mathbf{\le 100\text{ mW}}$ ($+20\text{ dBm}$) EIRP.
  • Pseudorandom Frequency Hopping: The P900 module is programmed via custom AT command registers (`AT&W`, `S102`) to restrict its pseudo-random hopping table strictly to 16 channels within $917.2\text{--}924.8\text{ MHz}$, ensuring compliance with ICAO Doc 9718 aeronautical spectrum mandates [4].

5.0 Coordinate Transformation (A5)

5.0 Coordinate Transformation & Frame Alignment Mathematics

WGS84 → UTM Zone 48N → Map Frame

5.1 Geodetic WGS84 to UTM Zone 48N Projection Pipeline

The UAV Scout’s onboard RTK-GNSS reports ellipsoidal geodetic coordinates $(\phi, \lambda, h)$ on the WGS84 reference ellipsoid ($a = 6378137.0\text{ m}, f = 1/298.257223563, e^2 = 2f - f^2$). Singapore lies in UTM Zone 48N with central meridian $\lambda_0 = 105^\circ\text{ E}$, scale factor $k_0 = 0.9996$, and false Easting $E_0 = 500{,}000\text{ m}$.

The ellipsoidal radius of curvature in the prime vertical $N(\phi)$ and meridional arc distance $M(\phi)$ are computed as:

$$N(\phi) = \frac{a}{\sqrt{1 - e^2 \sin^2\phi}}, \quad M(\phi) = a \int_0^\phi \frac{1 - e^2}{(1 - e^2 \sin^2\theta)^{3/2}} d\theta$$

With longitudinal offset $p = (\lambda - \lambda_0) \cos\phi$ (in radians), the projected Easting $E$ and Northing $N$ are derived via Transverse Mercator expansion:

$$E = E_0 + k_0 N(\phi) \left[ p + \frac{(1 - T + C)p^3}{6} + \frac{(5 - 18T + T^2 + 72C - 58e'^2)p^5}{120} \right]$$
$$N = N_0 + k_0 \left[ M(\phi) + N(\phi) \tan\phi \left( \frac{p^2}{2} + \frac{(5 - T + 9C + 4C^2)p^4}{24} + \frac{(61 - 58T + T^2 + 600C - 330e'^2)p^6}{720} \right) \right]$$

Where $T = \tan^2\phi$, $C = e'^2 \cos^2\phi$, and second eccentricity $e'^2 = \frac{e^2}{1 - e^2}$.

5.2 2D Affine Datum Rotation into Local Metric Map Frame

To transform projected UTM Zone 48N Easting and Northing into the Nav2 metric coordinate frame (`map` frame), where the origin $(0, 0)$ is aligned with the surveyed airside runway threshold datum $(E_{\text{datum}}, N_{\text{datum}})$ and rotated by the runway heading azimuth $\theta_{\text{datum}}$:

$$\begin{bmatrix} x_{\text{map}} \\ y_{\text{map}} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_{\text{datum}} & \sin\theta_{\text{datum}} \\ -\sin\theta_{\text{datum}} & \cos\theta_{\text{datum}} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} E - E_{\text{datum}} \\ N - N_{\text{datum}} \end{bmatrix}$$

This linear affine transformation executes in under $0.02\text{ ms}$ in `ugv_c2_bridge_node.py`, ensuring instantaneous goal injection into the Nav2 global planner without floating-point accumulation drift.

5.3 Bridging Simulated Frame Transforms to Future UM980 Single-Antenna RTK Driver

In Phase 1 simulation, the transform tree `map ➔ odom ➔ base_footprint` is maintained via the Gazebo Harmonic differential drive plugin and `robot_localization` EKF (Moore & Stouch, 2014 [9]). In Semester 2 physical hardware, this architecture transitions seamlessly to the Unicorecomm UM980 single-antenna triple-band RTK-GNSS module:

Single-Antenna RTK Heading Kinematics vs Dual-Antenna Baseline

Unlike dual-antenna GNSS systems that derive static heading from carrier-phase interferometry across a rigid physical baseline, the single-antenna UM980 provides position fixes ($\pm 0.01\text{ m}$) and Course-Over-Ground (COG) vectors while the robot is in motion. To eliminate heading degeneracy during stationary stops or on-the-spot skid turns, the global EKF integrates high-frequency ($100\text{ Hz}$) angular rates from the onboard 9-DOF IMU (Euler angle $\dot{\psi}$), while resetting gyro bias whenever the single-antenna RTK velocity vector satisfies $v_{\text{GNSS}} > 0.35\text{ m/s}$.

6.0 Safety Interlocks (A3, A6)

6.0 Fail-Safe Interlocks & Link Loss State Machine

Watchdog Interlocks • Tiered Failsafe

Operating autonomous robotic platforms on an active runway requires deterministic safety interlocks to prevent rogue navigation or runaway vehicles during RF link interruption:

6.1 Watchdog Monitor 6.1 Keep-Alive Heartbeat Frequency (1.0–2.0 Hz) and Watchdog Monitoring

The UGV C2 bridge executes an asynchronous watchdog timer. Each ingested telemetry heartbeat increments an internal monotonic sequence counter. If the sliding window time delta $\Delta t = t_{\text{curr}} - t_{\text{last\_rx}}$ exceeds $1.5\text{ s}$, the watchdog raises an RF degradation warning flag.

6.2 Tiered Response 6.2 Tiered Fail-Safe Responses: Standby Deceleration (t > 5.0 s) vs. Autonomous RTB (t > 30.0 s)

Standby Deceleration ($t > 5.0\text{ s}$): If heartbeat loss persists beyond $5.0\text{ s}$, the UGV halts forward velocity at $1.5\text{ m/s}^2$ controlled deceleration, holds position, engages motor holding torque, and activates flashing amber airside strobes while keeping local collision avoidance active.

Autonomous RTB ($t > 30.0\text{ s}$): If link interruption exceeds $30.0\text{ s}$, the UGV purges pending mission queues, aborts active debris search, and commands Nav2 to execute an autonomous Return-to-Base (RTB) navigation routine along surveyed taxiway corridors back to the Home Docking Bay at $(-25.0, 0.0)$.

7.3 Tooling Trade Study (Custom C2 HUD vs. Foxglove vs. RViz)

Informed by comparative industry robotics benchmarks (Marova / ReductStore, 2025 [18]), this trade study evaluates why the team engineered a dedicated, lightweight C2 Tactical HUD for the Raspberry Pi 5 instead of standard robotics visualizers:

Custom C2 Tactical HUD Phase 1 Deployed

< 4% CPU • ~45 MB RAM. Purpose-built for RPi 5 field station. Direct CycloneDDS listener, 2D radar, telemetry HUD cards, single-click FOD dispatch & reverse docking.

RViz 2 (ROS 2 Native) Ruled Out for C2

50–80% CPU on RPi 5. Heavy Qt6/OGRE 3D engine. Single-robot centric; lacks high-level C2 abstractions (no waypoint queue, no battery SOC, no vacuum toggle).

Foxglove Studio Phase 2 Target A

Selected for Tower Fleet C2. Web-native multi-seat monitoring, high-res satellite tile overlays, bidirectional action dispatch, custom layout panels.

Rerun.io Phase 2 Target B

Selected for Forensic Post-Mortem. Sub-millisecond timeline scrubbing across synchronized sensor streams for post-mission incident audits and model retraining.

Synthesis: For live airfield operations and tower control, Foxglove Studio is the superior choice due to its web-native multi-seat accessibility and bidirectional action dispatch. Conversely, Rerun.io is the designated tool for offline post-mission algorithmic forensics.
8.0 Roadmap (A6)

8.0 Identified Shortcomings & Semester 2 Field Roadmap

Technical Limitations • Hardware Procurement

8.1 Technical Limitations (Wi-Fi Range Cutoff, Pending RF Procurement & Base Datum Drift)

Rigorous engineering requires transparent acknowledgment of Phase 1 technical boundaries:

  • Wi-Fi Range Cutoff: The Phase 1 benchtop utilizes direct RJ45 Ethernet and a local 5 GHz Wi-Fi router. In outdoor airfield environments, 5 GHz Wi-Fi drops out beyond $\approx 80\text{ m}$ due to severe asphalt multipath and vegetative absorption, making it unsuitable for multi-kilometer runway coverage.
  • Pending RF Procurement: The Microhard P900 V2 transceivers are currently in the institutional procurement pipeline. RF serialization characteristics and link margins were validated via mathematical modeling and serial character device proxies (`/dev/ttyUSB0`) rather than physical over-the-air transmitters.
  • Base Datum Drift: The digital twin assumes an idealized, static geodetic origin. In real-world single-antenna RTK deployments, uncalibrated base station shifts or ionospheric scintillation can introduce centimeter-level global offsets between UAV detection coordinates and UGV local map frames unless tied to a surveyed benchmark.

8.2 Phase 2 Hardware Procurement & Integration: Microhard P900 V2 & Physical UM980 Module

In Semester 2, the communications subsystem will transition from Software-in-the-Loop simulation onto the physical rover platform:

1. Microhard P900 V2 Physical Mount & AT Locking

Mount two physical P900 V2 modules (one on UAV companion computer, one on UGV chassis). Execute firmware locks to restrict FHSS operation strictly to Singapore’s 917–925 MHz window with $\le 100\text{ mW}$ EIRP. Connect via hardware UART to NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano.

2. Unicorecomm UM980 Single-Antenna RTK Hardware Bridge

Deploy the physical UM980 single-antenna RTK receiver with high-gain active patch antenna and NTRIP RTCM 3.3 differential corrections via local 4G telemetry proxy or stationary base station, achieving true $\pm 1.0\text{ cm}$ georeferencing.

3. Full Outdoor Runway Trial Validation

Conduct end-to-end multi-agent runway clearance trials: UAV sweeps at $35\text{ km/h}$, detects seeded debris, dispatches C2 alert via P900 V2 over $2\text{ km}$ distance, UGV navigates autonomously, verifies debris, activates vacuum suction, and reports ledger updates back to the tower.
