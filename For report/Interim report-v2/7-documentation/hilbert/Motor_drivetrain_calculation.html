1. Operational Mission Profile & Vehicle Boundary Parameters
System Constraints

The powertrain sizing derives directly from the operational requirements of airfield Foreign Object Debris (FOD) inspection. The UGV operates under stringent time windows between aircraft takeoffs and landings, requiring sustained transit speeds of at least $10\text{ km/h}$ ($2.78\text{ m/s}$), rapid acceleration, and steady torque up airport drainage perimeter ramps.

2. Kinematic Velocity & Wheel Rotational Speed (RPM) Derivation
Kinematic Model

For a drive wheel with radius $r = 0.10\text{ m}$ in pure rolling contact without excessive longitudinal slip ($\kappa \le 5\%$), the vehicle linear velocity $v$ and wheel rotational angular velocity $\omega$ are related by:

$$v = \omega \cdot r \implies \omega = \frac{v}{r}$$

At the target cruise speed of $10\text{ km/h} \approx 2.778\text{ m/s}$:

$$\omega_{\text{cruise}} = \frac{2.778\text{ m/s}}{0.10\text{ m}} = 27.78\text{ rad/s}$$

Converting angular velocity to revolutions per minute (RPM):

$$N_{\text{wheel}} = \frac{\omega \cdot 60}{2\pi} = \frac{27.78 \cdot 60}{2 \cdot 3.14159} = \frac{1666.8}{6.2832} \approx 265.28\text{ RPM}$$

At the peak design speed $v_{\max} = 12\text{ km/h} = 3.333\text{ m/s}$:

$$N_{\max} = \frac{3.333}{0.10} \cdot \frac{60}{2\pi} = 33.33 \cdot 9.5493 \approx 318.3\text{ RPM}$$
Motor RPM Specification: The selected hub motor must have a rated speed of at least $300\text{ RPM}$ and a peak no-load speed $\ge 350\text{ RPM}$ at nominal $48\text{V}$ bus voltage to maintain speed headroom under battery discharge down to $42.0\text{V}$.
3. Longitudinal Tractive Effort & Comprehensive Torque Budget
Dynamic Force Analysis

The total tractive force $F_{\text{tractive}}$ required at the wheel contact patch is governed by the longitudinal equation of motion:

$$F_{\text{tractive}} = F_{\text{grade}} + F_{\text{rolling}} + F_{\text{acc}} + F_{\text{aero}}$$

1. Incline Grade Resistance ($F_{\text{grade}}$)

Component of gravitational force acting parallel to the $15^\circ$ slope:

$$F_{\text{grade}} = M \cdot g \cdot \sin(\theta)$$

• Fully-Laden ($65\text{ kg}$): $65 \cdot 9.81 \cdot \sin(15^\circ) = \mathbf{165.0\text{ N}}$
• Nominal ($40\text{ kg}$): $40 \cdot 9.81 \cdot \sin(15^\circ) = \mathbf{101.5\text{ N}}$

2. Rolling Resistance ($F_{\text{rolling}}$)

Hysteresis energy loss in pneumatic tire carcass on asphalt ($C_{rr} = 0.030$):

$$F_{\text{rolling}} = C_{rr} \cdot M \cdot g \cdot \cos(\theta)$$

• Fully-Laden On Slope: $0.03 \cdot 65 \cdot 9.81 \cdot \cos(15^\circ) = \mathbf{18.48\text{ N}}$
• Flat Asphalt ($\theta=0^\circ$): $0.03 \cdot 65 \cdot 9.81 = \mathbf{19.13\text{ N}}$

3. Inertial Acceleration ($F_{\text{acc}}$)

Overcoming linear and rotational wheel inertia ($a = 0.80\text{ m/s}^2$):

$$F_{\text{acc}} = M_{\text{eff}} \cdot a \quad (M_{\text{eff}} \approx 1.05 M)$$

• Fully-Laden: $1.05 \cdot 65 \cdot 0.80 = \mathbf{54.60\text{ N}}$
• Nominal ($40\text{ kg}$): $1.05 \cdot 40 \cdot 0.80 = \mathbf{33.60\text{ N}}$

4. Aerodynamic Drag ($F_{\text{aero}}$)

Air resistance at cruise ($v = 2.78\text{ m/s}$, air density $\rho = 1.204\text{ kg/m}^3$):

$$F_{\text{aero}} = \frac{1}{2} \rho \cdot (C_d A) \cdot (v + v_{\text{wind}})^2$$

• Still air ($v=2.78\text{ m/s}$): $0.5 \cdot 1.204 \cdot 0.28 \cdot 2.78^2 = \mathbf{1.30\text{ N}}$
• $5\text{ m/s}$ Headwind: $0.5 \cdot 1.204 \cdot 0.28 \cdot (7.78)^2 = \mathbf{10.20\text{ N}}$

4. Electrical Power, Bus Voltage & Battery Discharge Sizing
48V LiFePO4 Bus

The electrical input power $P_{\text{elec}}$ required from the 48V power bus is determined by mechanical power $P_{\text{mech}} = \tau \cdot \omega$ and powertrain system efficiency $\eta_{\text{system}} = \eta_{\text{inverter}} \cdot \eta_{\text{motor}} \approx 0.95 \times 0.90 = 0.855$:

$$P_{\text{elec}} = \frac{P_{\text{mech}}}{\eta_{\text{system}}} = \frac{\tau \cdot \omega}{\eta_{\text{system}}}$$
Continuous Cruise Power

$P_{\text{mech,cruise}} = 2.04\text{ N}\cdot\text{m} \times 27.78\text{ rad/s} = 56.67\text{ W}$
Electrical Power $P_{\text{elec}} = \frac{56.67}{0.855} \approx 66.3\text{ W}$
Bus Current ($48\text{V}$): $I_{\text{bus}} = \frac{66.3}{48} = \mathbf{1.38\text{ A}}$

Peak Dynamic Power ($15^\circ$ Ramp)

$P_{\text{mech,peak}} = 21.89\text{ N}\cdot\text{m} \times 27.78\text{ rad/s} = 608.1\text{ W}$
Electrical Power $P_{\text{elec}} = \frac{608.1}{0.855} \approx 711.2\text{ W}$
Bus Current ($48\text{V}$): $I_{\text{bus,peak}} = \frac{711.2}{48} = \mathbf{14.82\text{ A}}$

Battery Pack Endurance

Pack Capacity: $48\text{V } 20\text{Ah} = 960\text{ W}\cdot\text{h}$
Combined Payload (Motors + Vacuum + Compute $\approx 450\text{ W}$):
Mission Endurance: $t_{\text{run}} = \frac{960\text{ Wh} \times 0.85}{450\text{ W}} \approx \mathbf{1.81\text{ hours}}$

5. Selected Hardware Specifications & Architectural Rationale
Hardware Bill of Materials

Rather than using traditional brushed DC or BLDC motors paired with planetary gearheads (which suffer from mechanical backlash, grease degradation, gear tooth shear under runway vibration, and lower efficiency), the platform adopts Direct-Drive High-Torque Hub Motors:

Dual ZLLG80ASM25 0-HT V2.0 Direct-Drive Hubs

  • Rated Voltage: $48\text{V DC}$ (Operational range $36 - 60\text{V}$).
  • Rated Speed: $300\text{ RPM}$ ($\approx 11.3\text{ km/h}$ on $200\text{ mm}$ tire).
  • Rated Torque: $15.0\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ (continuous thermal rating).
  • Peak Stall Torque: $28.0\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ ($>2.3\times$ safety margin over worst-case incline launch).
  • Rated Power: $450\text{ W}$ per motor ($900\text{ W}$ combined dual drive).
  • Encoder Feedback: Built-in 1024-line quadrature incremental optical encoder ($4096\text{ counts/rev}$ with $4\times$ decoding), yielding $0.153\text{ mm}$ position resolution at tire tread.
  • Environmental Rating: IP65 sealed housing to prevent tarmac dust, water splash, and loose bitumen aggregate ingress.

ZLAC8015D V4.2 Dual-Channel Servo Driver

  • Architecture: Dual-channel synchronized Field-Oriented Control (FOC) sinusoidal vector drive.
  • Continuous Output Current: $15\text{ A}$ continuous per channel ($30\text{ A}$ peak).
  • Communications Protocol: CANopen (CiA 402 device profile, up to $1\text{ Mbps}$) with secondary RS485 Modbus RTU interface.
  • ROS 2 Integration: Direct interface with ROS 2 ros2_control hardware driver publishing joint states at $50\text{ Hz}$ and subscribing to wheel velocity commands.
  • Protection Features: Overvoltage, undervoltage, thermal foldback at $85^\circ\text{C}$, stator phase short protection, and regenerative braking voltage clamping.
Direct Drive Advantage: Zero gearbox backlash enables instantaneous speed and directional reversal without driveline slop, which is crucial for high-accuracy local SLAM odometry estimation and centimetre-level in-place turning over detected FOD targets.
6. Emergency Braking Distance & Tire Adhesion Limits
Safety Verification

To prevent collision with runway infrastructure or airside vehicles upon emergency obstacle detection by the 3D LiDAR, the braking distance must remain within $1.5\text{ m}$ from full cruise speed ($10\text{ km/h} = 2.78\text{ m/s}$).

Assuming an asphalt friction coefficient $\mu = 0.75$ and maximum regenerative dynamic braking deceleration $a_{\text{brake}} = 3.5\text{ m/s}^2$ (well below the tire skid limit $\mu g \approx 7.35\text{ m/s}^2$):

$$d_{\text{stop}} = \frac{v^2}{2 \cdot a_{\text{brake}}} = \frac{(2.778)^2}{2 \cdot 3.5} = \frac{7.716}{7.0} = \mathbf{1.10\text{ metres}}$$

Accounting for a combined LiDAR detection cycle latency and ROS 2 emergency stop message broadcast delay of $t_{\text{react}} \approx 100\text{ ms}$:

$$d_{\text{total,stop}} = v \cdot t_{\text{react}} + d_{\text{stop}} = (2.778 \times 0.10) + 1.10 = 0.28 + 1.10 = \mathbf{1.38\text{ metres}}$$

The total emergency stop distance of $1.38\text{ m}$ comfortably satisfies the $2.0\text{ m}$ obstacle stop boundary maintained by the Nav2 costmap local obstacle safety layer.
