One cycle

Two Z steppers lift, one stepper drives the blade. The scraper floats in the blade's slots; the cycle lifts it clear on the way out and lets it drop to sweep. Tap a step to jump there.

    The new part

    Box to bin hose

    The hose replaces v1's drop door and chute. Gravity still does the moving; the hose just keeps it enclosed.

    WhatValueWhy it matters
    Hose run, docked–Clamp land to clamp land, straight at 35° downhill. FOD slides on the hose floor into the bin.
    Hose run, box on the asphalt–The hose has to stretch this far when the box is fully down. Buy a stretch hose rated for it, or stop the lift higher.
    Air speed in the hose~3.5 m/sYour vacuum's ~45 L/s through a 127 mm bore. Enough to carry dust and feathers. A washer needs ~15 m/s to lift, so it relies on the 35° fall.
    Clamps2 × 130–150 mmWorm-drive band clamps, 12.5 mm band. Each end has a raised bead the hose stretches over and a flat land for the band.
    Hose grip on the spigots22 mm / 40 mmBox end / bin end. The bin elbow has a stop shoulder the hose butts against.

    The animation bends the hose as a smooth curve and lets it stretch. A real corrugated hose sags and twists a little more than this.

    Open issues

    What to watch

    IssueStatusDetail
    Hose cuff at full-downRubsWith the box on the asphalt the 35° outlet points the hose cuff about 1 mm into the ground. Stop the lift ~5 mm higher, or turn the outlet bend down to ~20°.
    Hose stretchCheckSee the two hose lengths above. A plain corrugated hose won't double its length.
    Washers under the scraperTestThe scraper floats on its own weight with a TPU edge. A thin washer can slip under on rough asphalt. Test before printing the rest.
    Overhang on the ball nutsCheckThe box hangs forward of the lead screws, so the nuts carry a bending moment. Linear guides on the columns would take it off the screws.
    Fall into the binFine35° is above the ~27° slide angle for PETG and dry feathers. A damp bird is slower but still slides.
