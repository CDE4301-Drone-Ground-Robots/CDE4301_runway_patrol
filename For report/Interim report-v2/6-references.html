[1] Academic Journal 2026

Digitalization and Automation of Runway Inspection Using Unmanned Aerial Vehicles ↗

Authors: Krestenitis, M., Petropoulos, A., Koulalis, I., Stipanovic, I., Skaric Palic, S., Ioannidis, K., & Vrochidis, S.
Source: Sensors, Vol. 26, Issue 4, Article 1100, pp. 1–17.

Contribution to Project: Provides mathematical frameworks for UAV aerial flight path optimization across active runways. Demonstrates that airborne high-resolution photogrammetry captures macro surface anomalies at $30\text{--}40\text{ km/h}$, significantly reducing Runway Occupancy Time (ROT) compared to human walking or driving patrols.
[2] Review Paper 2025
DOI: 10.3390/rs17020225 ↗

A Review of Foreign Object Debris Detection on Airport Runways: Sensors and Algorithms ↗

Authors: Shan, J., Miccinesi, L., Beni, A., Pagnini, L., Cioncolini, A., & Pieraccini, M.
Source: Remote Sensing, Vol. 17, Issue 2, Article 225, pp. 1–33.

Contribution to Project: Synthesizes empirical detection ranges and environmental vulnerability across millimeter-wave radar, LiDAR, and computer vision. Served as the basis for our Multi-Modal Sensor Fusion architecture—combining LiDAR elevation profiles to reject flat optical artifacts (tire marks, asphalt joints) from 3D physical debris.
[3] University Design Report 2016
UC Berkeley / FAA ACRP ↗

Drone-enabled Foreign Object Debris (FOD) Removal System in ad hoc Situations ↗

Authors: Yamamoto, H., Sattar, R. A., Wang, J., Mendez, L. F., & Rakas, J.
Source: Airport Cooperative Research Program (ACRP) University Design Competition Report, University of California, Berkeley.

Contribution to Project: Proved that attempting physical debris retrieval purely using multi-rotor UAVs creates severe rotor downwash (scattering light debris), high motor strain, and rapid battery depletion. Directly motivated FOD Hunter's division of labor: lightweight UAV for aerial scouting, heavy UGV for targeted physical suction.
[4] Commercial SOTA 2024
ST Engineering / CAG ↗

iFerret™ 2.0 AI-Powered Foreign Object Debris Detection System ↗

Authors: Stratec Intelligent Vision / NCS / Changi Airport Group.
Source: Technical Product Datasheet & Airside Deployment Documentation, Singapore Changi Airport.

Contribution to Project: Benchmarked the industry gold standard for electro-optical automated FOD detection currently protecting Singapore Changi Airport. Highlighted the primary operational bottleneck: fixed optical cameras cannot physically pick up debris and generate elevated false alarm rates during heavy tropical rainstorms and night shadows.
[5] Regulatory Standard 2014
FAA AC 150/5220-24 ↗

Airport Foreign Object Debris (FOD) Management Equipment ↗

Issuing Body: Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), U.S. Department of Transportation.
Standard: Advisory Circular AC 150/5220-24, Airport Safety & Operations Division.

Contribution to Project: Serves as the primary regulatory performance target for our vision and sensor subsystems. Defines standard target geometries ($\varnothing 38\text{ mm} \times 31\text{ mm}$ metal cylinder, $20\text{ mm}$ optical cube), minimum detection ranges, scan intervals, and mobile vehicle velocity requirements ($48\text{ km/h}$).
[6] ASTM Standard 2020
ASTM D5340-20 ↗

Standard Test Method for Airport Pavement Condition Index (PCI) Surveys ↗

Issuing Body: ASTM International.
Standard: ASTM D5340-20, Book of Standards, Road and Paving Materials.

Contribution to Project: Provides the classification framework for FOD Hunter's runway structural defect inspection module. Categorizes distress types into alligator cracking, block cracking, joint seal damage, and asphalt spalling, enabling georeferenced severity logging.
